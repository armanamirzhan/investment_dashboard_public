SMR — Advanced Nuclear

Why nuclear shows up in the AI power story, how merchant plants and offtake contracts fit, and weekly scorecards for public reactor developers.

As of Oct 2, 2026

Emerging theme — nuclear for AI load

Near-term AI buildouts often hit nearer bottlenecks first: power equipment, packaging, and high-bandwidth memory (HBM). After those, the conversation often turns to 24/7 clean electricity — plants that run day and night without depending on weather. That is where nuclear power and small modular reactors (SMRs) enter the picture.

An SMR is a nuclear reactor built in factory-sized modules instead of one giant custom plant. The idea is shorter build times and the ability to add capacity in steps. Most SMRs are still in licensing or early construction; many AI campuses that need power this decade also look at existing large reactors (restarts, life extensions) and long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs) — contracts to buy electricity at agreed terms.

Merchant nuclear vs regulated utilities

A regulated utility usually earns a fixed allowed return set by state regulators. When power is scarce, that structure often does not let shareholders keep “scarcity rents” the way a competitive market plant can.

Merchant nuclear owners sell into wholesale markets or under private contracts. When AI and cloud companies (often called hyperscalers) sign long PPAs, that path can be cleaner for investors who want exposure to contracted offtake rather than regulated rate cases alone.

Useful public examples of the existing fleet + contracts story:

  • Constellation Energy (CEG) — large U.S. nuclear capacity, plus deals that restart or keep plants online for data-center and clean-power buyers (for example Microsoft’s Crane / Three Mile Island Unit 1 path). Calvert Cliffs–Amazon offtake is summarized on the Power Map.
  • Vistra (VST) — merchant generation with nuclear in the mix (including Comanche Peak) and wholesale / retail offtake exposure.
  • Talen Energy (TLN) — merchant nuclear highlighted by the Susquehanna–Amazon PPA path tying an existing plant to hyperscaler load.

That thesis is more about AI-driven power availability than about being a pure SMR equipment maker. Public commentary has often noted large re-ratings as AI power demand rose — treat price history as context, not a forecast.

Separately, utility combinations near dense data-center corridors (sometimes called “Data Center Alley” in Northern Virginia) also matter for controlled supply next to load. NextEra Energy (NEE) and Dominion Energy (D) announced a pending all-stock acquisition on May 18, 2026 (about $67 B equity value in deal coverage), still subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals (state utility commissions, FERC, NRC, antitrust). That is a pending merger, not a completed package or informal partnership. Capacity and “corridor GW” figures in secondary briefs vary — check primary filings rather than treating any one ~GW number as verified.

Ratepayer Protection Pledge (March 2026)

In March 2026 the White House published a voluntary Ratepayer Protection Pledge. In plain terms, large AI and cloud companies agree to build, bring, or buy the generation their data centers need, and to pay for related delivery upgrades, so ordinary household bills are not the first place AI load shows up.

That framing is supportive for nuclear and SMR offtake: if hyperscalers must bring new or additive supply, long nuclear PPAs and SMR campus deals become a natural matching product — alongside gas, renewables, and storage. The pledge is voluntary policy signaling, not a guarantee that any one project gets built.

Large reactors & SMR OEMs

Besides pure-play SMR developers, large reactor original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) matter for AI campuses that may buy conventional large reactors, SMRs, or both.

  • GE Vernova Hitachi BWRX-300 — boiling-water SMR design from GE Hitachi Nuclear Energy. Public equity exposure is mainly GE Vernova (GEV) plus Hitachi (6501.T). Ontario Power Generation’s Darlington SMR project took a final investment decision (FID) in May 2025; TVA’s Clinch River site remains an important U.S. licensing / siting reference.
  • Westinghouse (AP1000 large reactor / AP300 SMR) — the operating company is private. Public parents after the 2023 close: Cameco (CCJ) ~49% and Brookfield Renewable (BEP) ~51%. U.S. new-build and any IPO or listing talk should be treated carefully: policy partnerships and term sheets can change ownership economics without creating a liquid Westinghouse ticker. Not investment advice.

Selected nuclear supply chain

Heavy components and nuclear services names often cited alongside reactor OEMs: Doosan Enerbility (034020.KS), BWX Technologies (BWXT), and Curtiss-Wright (CW). These are suppliers / fabricators, not reactor developers.

Private developers

Many advanced-reactor companies are still private (no liquid public ticker). Useful names to watch in press and licensing dockets — without inventing tickers: TerraPower, Kairos Power, Holtec, Last Energy, Radiant, Antares, Valar Atomics, Aalo Atomics. Treat fundraising headlines as progress signals, not tradable equity unless a listing is confirmed.
