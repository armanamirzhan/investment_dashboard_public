What this is

This section was written by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, at the request of the site's owner. It is an independent second opinion that sits alongside the pages built by the Grok section bots. The work had three parts. First, three parallel audits read every page, diagram, figure and data file on the site, checking which relevant companies are missing, which tickers or facts are wrong, and which statements are out of date. They found 587 items, 110 of them high priority. Second, five research agents each wrote a dated, sourced report on one part of the value chain: power inside the data hall, power semiconductors, grid and facility equipment, lasers and photonic materials, and copper versus co-packaged optics. Third, I checked those reports against each other, pulled one consistent snapshot of prices and analyst consensus for about 140 listings, and made the final calls on the two pages below.

Both pages follow the same structure: a short analysis of the field, the engineering realities that I think the market has not priced, a bottleneck scorecard, a list of what is already priced or over-hyped, and then the picks. Each pick shows what the company makes, why that product matters, why this company, dated evidence, risks, the analyst consensus (rating with mean, high and low targets) and my own rating and 12-month target with its basis.

Five conclusions that cut across both fields

1. The market marked down the bottleneck owners while their order books grew

Many owners of the scarcest steps trade 30–55% below their mid-2026 highs: HD Hyundai Electric (transformers, −52%), Mitsui Kinzoku (copper foil for circuit boards, −53%), Nittobo (glass cloth for circuit boards, −47%), Powell (medium-voltage switchgear, −41%), onsemi (power chips, −40%), BE Semiconductor Industries, or BESI (chip-bonding tools, −40%), Aixtron (tools that grow lasers, −39%) and Infineon (power chips, −32%). Over the same period their backlogs and the third-party shortage estimates rose. Several things drove the shares down. Interest rates rose: the Federal Reserve raised its policy rate by a quarter point in September (CNBC), and the US 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.24% on 1 October 2026. A report on 18 August of about $3 trillion of off-balance-sheet AI commitments hit power-equipment shares (Yahoo Finance). On 14 September AI-lab leaders called for a development slowdown, and the PHLX Semiconductor Index, the main US index of chip stocks, fell 5.9% that day (Reuters via Investing.com). Money also rotated into memory stocks. Yet the semiconductor index is still up about 81% this year and only about 12% below its closing high (Yahoo Finance data), so the markdowns are concentrated in industrial and materials suppliers rather than in the chip leaders. That gap is where most of my picks come from.

2. Scarcity has moved upstream into materials, certification and tools

The binding constraints of 2026–28 are less often the famous systems and more often their inputs. In power they are extra-high-voltage transformers and their bushings (the insulated feed-throughs that carry conductors into the transformer), certified protection equipment that can safely interrupt direct current, electrical-code approval, and onsite gas engines. In interconnects they are ultra-smooth copper foil, low-expansion glass cloth, the indium-phosphide (InP) wafers that lasers for fiber links are built on, and the epitaxy tools that grow the lasers' crystal layers. Many of these owners are Japanese or Korean industrials with thin US analyst coverage and no liquid US share line. That is part of why they are mispriced, and also why they are less convenient to buy.

3. Both transitions are real, but slower than the narrative, and the slowness helps the suppliers

Direct-current power in the data hall arrives in three steps: "sidecar" racks in 2026, which sit beside the servers and convert alternating current to direct current for a whole row; native 800-volt racks in 2027–28; and facility-wide DC after about 2029, using solid-state transformers (power electronics that convert medium-voltage AC straight to DC). Optics in AI clusters also arrives in steps. Co-packaged optics, which puts the optical engine on the same package as the switch chip, is already shipping in scale-out switches, the network that connects racks. Optical links for scale-up, the links that join GPUs into one large system, come between racks in 2027–28, and inside the rack only in 2029–30. A slower transition extends the life of copper boards, which means more demand for foil, glass cloth and laminates. It also gives the power incumbents time to shift their product mix instead of losing a market.

4. The popular expressions are priced

GE Vernova (about 40× 2027 earnings), Bloom Energy (about 56×), Lumentum (at its high, about 30× next fiscal year's earnings and 48× this year's), Astera Labs (about 56×), Soitec (up about 580% this year) and the capacitor makers (Samsung Electro-Mechanics up about 520%) already price the story. Several popular narratives also run ahead of the engineering. Solid-state transformers will not earn meaningful revenue before about 2029. Optical versions of NVLink, NVIDIA's GPU-to-GPU link, will not reach inside the rack in 2027. Glass-core package substrates, which use glass instead of resin at the core of the chip package, have slipped beyond 2026–27. Hybrid bonding, which stacks chips copper-to-copper without solder, will not arrive with the HBM4 generation of stacked memory. And micro-LED links, which send data with arrays of tiny LEDs instead of lasers, will not replace copper soon.

5. The main risks are shared

Five risks hit both fields at once. The first is a pause in AI capital spending: the hyperscalers (the largest cloud companies) still guide to record 2026 spending, but September brought public calls for a slowdown. The second is higher interest rates. The third is a slip in NVIDIA's roadmap, such as its next rack design, Kyber, moving to 2028. The fourth is China policy: export licensing of indium, which laser makers need; a suspension of China's gallium export ban that expires around late November 2026; a trade truce extended only to 10 January 2027; and the Pentagon's Section 1260H list of "Chinese military companies", which now includes the largest optical-transceiver maker, InnoLight. The fifth is a capacity overbuild in 2028–29 that could hit transformers, indium-phosphide wafers, low-power lasers and silicon-photonics foundries (the plants that make optical chips in silicon).

What the site audit found

The audits read every page, every clickable diagram stage and every data file, and checked time-sensitive facts against primary sources where they could. They produced 587 items (110 high, 253 medium and 224 low priority), each with the file, the exact location, the action (add, fix, move, remove or link), the reason and a source. The most important are below. The full list is in coverage-gaps.json, and the notes for the Grok team explain how to use it.

  • Wrong tickers or links.
    • ABB is shown as NYSE-listed and linked to Fidelity as "ABB". It trades on the SIX Swiss Exchange, and its US over-the-counter line is ABBNY.
    • Schneider Electric's "SNEXF" does not exist; its US over-the-counter lines are SBGSY and SBGSF.
    • SCREEN Holdings is shown as 6305.T, which is Hitachi Construction Machinery; SCREEN is 7735.T.
    • SK Siltron is shown as 008500.KS, a car-seat-fabric maker; SK Siltron is unlisted.
    • The Fidelity link for ASM International opens a silver miner; it should use ASMIY.
    • The Power Map's link builder turns exchange-prefixed tickers such as "LSE: CWR" (London) and "KRX: 336260" (Korea) into links for the exchange code instead of the company.
  • Corporate events not reflected.
    • New listings: Cerebras (CBRS), SK hynix's US share line (SKHY), SpaceX/xAI (SPCX) and Zhipu (2513.HK).
    • Changes of ownership: Altera is now standalone under Silver Lake, CoolIT belongs to Ecolab, DuPont's electronics business became Qnity (Q), Synopsys owns Ansys, and NVIDIA's purchase of Hugging Face is pending.
    • Wrong attributions: the Reinhausen solid-state-transformer partnership is Siemens AG's, not Siemens Energy's. ABB no longer supplies high-voltage direct-current (HVDC) links, since that business is now Hitachi Energy. The "NextEra–Dominion package" is an acquisition, not a partnership.
  • Stale or inconsistent data.
    • Every 2026 capital-spending figure on the Hyperscalers page is out of date. Current guidance is Amazon about $220 billion, Alphabet $195–205 billion, Meta $130–145 billion and Oracle's fiscal 2027 $90–95 billion.
    • Dollar amounts were stripped from text in the public data files ("Cloud revenue 0B").
    • The Oklo tracker shows stale gauges.
    • The master company file holds 87 records, while the pages show about 174 tickers.
  • Coverage gaps in core sections.
    • Power: no battery-storage stage at all, and missing generator-set makers (Cummins, Generac, Rolls-Royce mtu), Korean transformer makers, high-voltage cable makers and Lite-On.
    • Hardware: no circuit-board, laminate or copper-foil layer, even though it is the copper bottleneck.
    • Photonics: no Asian transceiver or laser makers (InnoLight, Eoptolink, Sumitomo Electric, Yuanjie) and no makers of the indium-phosphide wafers that lasers are built on. The co-packaged-optics pages show Broadcom's older Bailly switch instead of its current Tomahawk 6-Davisson.
    • Fabrication: missing packaging and test tools (DISCO, BESI, Hanmi, ASMPT), substrates (Ibiden, Unimicron, Ajinomoto) and Aixtron.
    • Software: missing major security and data-infrastructure names.
    • Hyperscalers: missing the Chinese clouds, most "neoclouds" (specialist GPU-cloud companies), the bitcoin miners that now lease power and space to AI customers, and the data-center real-estate investment trusts (REITs).

Method, data and caveats

Prices are the latest full-session close available on the morning of 2 October 2026, New York time: 1 October 2026 for US and European listings, 2 October 2026 for Tokyo, Seoul, Taipei and Hong Kong, and 30 September 2026 for mainland China (closed for the National Day holiday). Market values are converted to US dollars at mid-morning New York exchange rates on 2 October 2026.

Independent ratings and targets come from the Yahoo Finance consensus feed, which carries data from LSEG (the London Stock Exchange Group) and S&P Global, retrieved on 2 October 2026. The rating mean runs from 1 (strong buy) to 5 (sell); "No label" means the feed showed no rating although targets exist. Consensus targets for Asian companies can lag recent broker changes. "From high" is the distance below the highest daily close of the past 52 weeks.

Claude's 12-month target is a stated multiple multiplied by next fiscal year's consensus earnings per share, rounded. The fair-value range is ±10% around it. Choosing the multiple is the judgment call, explained in each card: I use lower multiples where earnings look like a cyclical peak, higher ones for durable franchises and for cyclical companies whose earnings are still near the bottom of their cycle, and I took into account a 10-year US Treasury yield of 5.24%. "Would buy below" applies the same method with a stricter multiple. Ratings: Buy = high conviction that the shares are worth more than the price; Accumulate = positive, but with more risk or less upside than a Buy; Speculative buy = attractive only if a risky part of the thesis works, with a real chance of large losses; Hold = fairly priced; Avoid = the risks or the price outweigh the thesis. A rating combines the upside with my conviction and the risks, so a low-risk Buy can show less upside than an Accumulate.

Research process. Five research agents each wrote a value-chain report: in-hall DC power; power semiconductors; grid and facility equipment; lasers and photonic materials; and copper, co-packaged optics and optical I/O. Each report gives every figure with its date and source. I then checked the reports against each other, re-pulled all prices and consensus data in one consistent snapshot, and made the final calls myself. Figures marked "my estimate" are my arithmetic on stated assumptions. Sources marked "secondary" are press relays or aggregators. The full reports are in this repository under claude-summary/research/.
