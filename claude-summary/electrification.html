1. The short version

The market split the AI power trade in two during 2026. The equipment that everyone already associates with AI power, such as liquid cooling, uninterruptible power supplies and the largest electrical manufacturers, trades at roughly 25 to 40 times next year's earnings. Meanwhile, several companies that own the scarcest steps in the chain fell 30–55% from their mid-2026 highs. Those steps are extra-high-voltage transformers, engineered medium-voltage switchgear and the power semiconductors for 800-volt racks. The shares fell on fund flows, on interest rates (the US 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.24% on 1 October 2026, two weeks after the Federal Reserve raised its policy rate by a quarter point [X-20]) and on fears that AI spending would slow, even though the companies' orders and backlogs kept rising. The second-largest rack-power vendor, for its part, trades at about two-thirds of the leader's earnings multiple. Most of my picks come from these gaps between order books and share prices.

The move to direct current (DC) inside the data hall is real, but it is slower than the headlines suggest and it moves money differently. 2026 is mostly a year of ±400-volt "sidecar" power racks: cabinets that sit next to the server racks and convert the hall's ordinary alternating current (AC) to DC for a whole row. Native 800-volt racks ramp in 2027–28, or later if NVIDIA's Kyber rack (its next rack design, drawing several hundred kilowatts) slips to 2028 as has been reported. Facility-wide DC and solid-state transformers, which are power electronics that convert medium-voltage AC straight to DC and replace both an iron-core transformer and a rectifier, are mainly a 2029-and-later story. When the power supply inside each server disappears, its dollars do not vanish. They move to power racks, battery backup, high-ratio DC-to-DC converters and DC protection, and mostly to the same two Taiwanese vendors, Delta Electronics and Lite-On, and to the chipmakers that sell power switches made of silicon and of two newer materials, silicon carbide and gallium nitride, which handle high voltages more efficiently.

The scarcest items sit in three places most investors are not looking:

  • the top of the voltage stack: 345–765 kilovolt transformers, high-voltage bushings (the insulated feed-throughs that carry conductors through a transformer's steel tank) and breakers, scarce until 2028–29;
  • certified DC protection and electrical-code approval for 800-volt halls, since the US electrical code adds full 800-volt DC support only in its 2029 cycle;
  • delivering about 3,000 amperes into each GPU package, an area that is now in patent litigation.

The equipment that makes the electricity (gas turbines, engines, fuel cells and nuclear reactors) is covered on the generation page, and the utilities, merchant generators and pipeline owners that sell the electricity on the energy-provider page.

2. How electricity reaches the GPU, and where the money moves as the hall goes DC

In a conventional AI data center, utility power arrives at a substation where large transformers step it down from transmission voltage, for example 345 kilovolts (kV), to medium voltage, typically 13.8–34.5 kV. Medium-voltage switchgear, cabinets of breakers and switches, distributes and protects that power. Facility transformers then step it down again to 480 or 415 volts AC. A double-conversion uninterruptible power supply (UPS) converts it to DC to charge batteries and back to AC, so the load never sees a grid disturbance. Power distribution units (PDUs) carry it to the racks. Inside every server, an AC-to-DC power supply produces a 54-volt bus, and DC-to-DC converters and voltage regulators finally step it down to below one volt at the GPU. Each conversion wastes a few percent of the power, and SemiAnalysis, an independent semiconductor research firm, models the whole chain at about 82% efficiency from grid to chip [P1-8].

The DC architecture removes stages. A power rack, also called a sidecar, converts AC to ±400 V or 800 V DC once for a whole row. The per-server power supplies disappear, and battery backup units attach directly to the DC bus. Later, a solid-state transformer, which is power electronics switching at tens of kilohertz instead of an iron-core transformer running at 60 hertz, converts medium-voltage AC straight to 800 V DC. Modelled efficiency rises to roughly 86–87%. Lower electricity bills are not the main prize, since the saving is only about $20–35 million a year per gigawatt (my estimate). What matters is that 3–5% more IT load (the power drawn by the servers, storage and network equipment themselves) fits on every gigawatt of scarce grid connection. At an assumed $30–40 billion of capital spending per gigawatt, that is about $1–2 billion more deployable compute per gigawatt (my estimate).

There are two DC standards, and both will coexist in the same halls for years. The hyperscalers (Google, Meta and Microsoft) chose ±400 V bipolar distribution (two conductors at plus and minus 400 volts relative to ground, so 800 volts between them) under the Open Compute Project (OCP) "Mt Diablo" and "Diablo 400" specifications. They chose it deliberately to reuse the electric-vehicle supply chain [P1-23]. NVIDIA chose 800 V monopolar distribution (one conductor at 800 volts plus a return) for its Kyber-generation racks [P1-26]. Suppliers with products for both standards, such as Delta, Lite-On and Infineon, are better placed than single-standard bets.

3. Six engineering realities the market has not fully priced

3.1 Energy storage moves closer to the racks; it does not disappear

A popular shorthand says that 800 V DC "kills the UPS". It kills the double-conversion UPS topology, not the storage function, and the amount of storage per megawatt goes up. NVIDIA's current GB300 (Blackwell Ultra) racks already hold 65 joules per GPU in the capacitors of their power shelves. Together with power caps and ramp limits, that cuts peak grid demand by about 30% in NVIDIA's own test [P1-20]. NVIDIA's 800 V architecture adds supercapacitors near the racks and battery systems at the utility connection [P1-19].

Grid operators are making this compulsory. The North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC) issued a Level 3 alert, its highest level, which requires specific actions from utilities and grid operators, in May 2026 after data centers dropped off the grid during faults [P3-86]. PJM, the grid operator for 13 eastern US states and Washington, DC, saw 3.8 GW of data-center load trip offline on 22 July 2026 [P3-87]. Reliability standards ordered by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) are due by 31 December 2026. Ride-through (staying connected through a brief grid fault) and power smoothing (evening out the rapid swings in an AI cluster's power draw) are becoming compliance spending, not optional resilience.

The beneficiaries are the rack-power integrators that sell battery backup units (Delta, Lite-On) and the medium-voltage UPS makers. The capacitor and cell suppliers are not, because their boom and bust already happened in 2026: many are 44–68% below their June highs.

3.2 The last centimeter is the physics bottleneck

A GPU of NVIDIA's next generation (Rubin) dissipating about 2.3 kW at a core voltage near 0.75 V draws roughly 3,000 amperes. If that current crossed a circuit board sideways through only 50 micro-ohms of resistance, the loss would be 3,000² × 50×10⁻⁶ ≈ 450 watts, about a fifth of the chip's own power (my calculation). So voltage regulators move under the package, a technique called vertical power delivery. There, current density per square millimetre is the binding constraint. Vicor claims 3 amperes per mm² now and 5 A/mm² in early 2027, against "barely" 1 A/mm² for integrated voltage regulators built into the chip package. That is a company claim, but it shows that the problem is one of physics, not factory capacity [P1-13].

The intellectual property here is in active litigation. Vicor's complaints at the US International Trade Commission (ITC) name almost the entire first-generation supply chain, including Delta, Monolithic Power Systems, Infineon, Flex, Celestica, Quanta, Foxconn and Luxshare. One exclusion order, which bars infringing products from import into the US, has already been issued (February 2025), and a new complaint was filed on 9 September 2026 [P1-14] [P1-15]. Few investors discuss this risk for Monolithic Power Systems, which trades at about 39 times 2027 earnings.

3.3 The hyperscaler DC standard is built on the electric-vehicle supply chain, so do not pay for chip pricing power

Because ±400 V reuses 650 V and 1,200 V electric-vehicle parts, 800 V is deflationary for silicon-carbide (SiC) and gallium-nitride (GaN) prices. At the crystal and substrate layer, SiC is in glut. Chinese 6-inch substrates fell to about $400 or less, and Yole Group, a semiconductor market-research firm, put upstream utilization near 50%, with the downturn lasting into 2027–28 [P2-49] [P2-47]. At the layer of finished devices that customers have qualified, supply is tight. Infineon says AI power demand "exceeds available supply… first come, first served" [P2-3], and onsemi is at 83% utilization and pushing a second price rise [P2-10].

The content is what grows. Power-semiconductor dollars per megawatt rise by about 60% at the midpoint, and the high-voltage share rises from about 30% to about 50% (onsemi: about $15,000 per rack today against more than $115,000 in an 800 V rack; Infineon: $100–250 per kW, $175 on average) [P2-11] [P2-2]. Device makers that buy substrates from many suppliers capture the spread between cheap wafers and tight devices. Captive crystal growers and GaN small caps do not.

3.4 Electrical codes and certified protection gate facility-wide DC

An arc in a DC circuit does not extinguish itself, because the current never passes through zero as it does in AC. Interrupting 800 V therefore needs either long mechanical arc chutes or a solid-state circuit breaker that opens in microseconds. Full support for 800 V DC in the US National Electrical Code is targeted only for the 2029 cycle. The safety standard NFPA 70E has no protective-equipment tables for 600–1,000 V DC, and the arc-flash standard IEEE 1584 does not cover DC [P1-8]. Until then each site needs case-by-case approval from the local electrical authority. Certified products are therefore scarce credentials. ABB's SACE Infinitus is described as the first solid-state breaker certified under the international (IEC) circuit-breaker standard 60947-2 (1,250 V DC, 2,500 A, under 25 microseconds) [P1-28]. LS Electric holds the first 1,500 V DC molded-case breaker (a compact breaker type used in switchboards) certified by UL, the main US product-safety certifier, and Eaton's medium-voltage solid-state transformer has IEC certification [P1-31].

3.5 Solid-state transformers are over-hyped for 2026–28 and under-appreciated for 2029–32

In May 2026 the number of commercial-class solid-state transformers in revenue service worldwide was "measured in single digits", and no vendor had UL data-center certification [P1-16] [P1-8]. They cost about twice as much as a conventional transformer. Their efficiency is only about 0.5–1.5 points better than a transformer followed by a rectifier, the conventional AC-to-DC converter (my estimate). Their medium-frequency transformer needs roughly three times the insulation [P1-17]. The semiconductor content is small, about $8–12 per kW of silicon carbide [P2-39], so a solid-state transformer is an integration and certification problem, not a chip shortage.

When they do scale, after about 2029, they replace the medium-to-low-voltage distribution transformers and low-voltage switchboards in new DC halls, not high-voltage substations. On a five-year view that is a risk for distribution-transformer specialists such as Hammond Power and the distribution segment of Forgent. It is not a risk for the extra-high-voltage makers.

3.6 The big-iron bottleneck has narrowed to the top of the voltage stack, and US tariffs are inverted

McKinsey estimates that Europe and North America had a 38% transformer shortfall in 2025. It expects the backlog to reach 3.3 times annual supply by 2030, with lead times of up to five years for the largest units [P3-5]. Survey averages are 128 weeks for power transformers and 144 weeks for generator step-up units [P3-1]. Below about 10 MVA (megavolt-amperes, roughly megawatts of transformer capacity), however, the market is loosening. Hammond's backlog fell 6.9% in a quarter as shipments exceeded orders, and tap-changers, the switches that adjust a transformer's voltage ratio, now ship in 16 weeks [P3-56] [P3-55]. The new choke point is high-voltage bushings. They have lead times of up to two years, and the first US-made 765 kV dry-type bushings arrive only in early 2028 [P3-54].

A tariff detail I have not seen discussed by investors favors makers with US factories. Under the 9 April 2026 Section 232 proclamation (US tariffs set on national-security grounds), imported grain-oriented electrical steel pays 50% and cores pay 25%. Finished liquid-filled transformers above 10 MVA pay only 15% until 31 December 2027, and then 25% [P3-45]. That implies a rush of imports in 2027, followed by pricing power from 2028 for transformers built in the US. Plants that qualify include HD Hyundai Electric's in Alabama, Hyosung's in Memphis, Siemens Energy's in Charlotte (2027) and Hitachi Energy's in South Boston (2028). Yet HD Hyundai Electric and Hyosung trade 52% and 39% below their 52-week closing highs, and LS Electric 34%.

Method, data and caveats

Prices. Prices are the latest full-session closes available on the morning of 2 October 2026, New York time: 1 October 2026 for US and European listings, 2 October 2026 for Tokyo, Seoul, Taipei and Hong Kong, and 30 September 2026 for mainland China (closed for the National Day holiday). Market values are converted to US dollars at mid-morning New York exchange rates on 2 October 2026.

Independent ratings and targets come from the Yahoo Finance consensus feed, which carries data from LSEG (the London Stock Exchange Group) and S&P Global, retrieved on 2 October 2026. The rating mean runs from 1 (strong buy) to 5 (sell); "No label" means the feed showed no rating although targets exist. Consensus targets for Asian companies can lag recent broker changes. ABB's consensus earnings are reported in US dollars and were converted to Swiss francs for its multiple. "From high" is the distance below the highest daily close of the past 52 weeks.

Claude's 12-month target is a stated multiple multiplied by next fiscal year's consensus earnings per share, rounded. The fair-value range is ±10% around it. Choosing the multiple is the judgment call, explained in each card: I use lower multiples where earnings look like a cyclical peak, higher ones for durable franchises and for cyclical companies whose earnings are still near the bottom of their cycle, and I took into account a 10-year US Treasury yield of 5.24% (1 October 2026 close). "Would buy below" applies the same method with a stricter multiple, rounded down to a round number (the nearest dollar below $100, the nearest $5 below $1,000, and so on). Ratings: Buy = high conviction that the shares are worth more than the price; Accumulate = positive, but with more risk or less upside than a Buy; Speculative buy = attractive only if a risky part of the thesis works, with a real chance of large losses; Hold = fairly priced; Avoid = the risks or the price outweigh the thesis. A rating combines the upside with my conviction and the risks, so a low-risk Buy can show less upside than an Accumulate.

Glossary

Short explanations of the technical and financial terms used on this page.

AC and DC
Alternating current reverses direction many times a second (60 times in the US) and is what the grid delivers. Direct current flows one way and is what chips use, so every data center converts AC to DC somewhere.
kV, MW, GW, MVA
A kilovolt (kV) is 1,000 volts. A megawatt (MW) is a million watts, and a gigawatt (GW) is 1,000 MW, roughly the output of a large power plant. Megavolt-amperes (MVA) measure transformer capacity and are roughly equal to megawatts.
High, medium and low voltage
On this page, high or extra-high voltage means transmission levels of about 115–765 kV; medium voltage is about 1–35 kV, used inside a campus; low voltage is below 1,000 V, used inside the hall.
Large power transformer, generator step-up unit
Large power transformers connect transmission lines to substations. Generator step-up units raise a power plant's output to transmission voltage. Both are built to order and take years to deliver.
Bushing
The insulated feed-through that carries a high-voltage conductor through a transformer's grounded steel tank.
Tap-changer
A switch on a transformer that adjusts its voltage ratio while it is running.
Switchgear
Cabinets of circuit breakers and switches that distribute and protect power. Gas-insulated switchgear is a compact high-voltage version.
Rectifier
A device that converts AC to DC.
Solid-state transformer
Power electronics, switching at tens of kilohertz instead of 60 hertz, that convert medium-voltage AC directly to DC. One unit replaces an iron-core transformer and a rectifier.
UPS and double conversion
An uninterruptible power supply keeps power flowing during grid disturbances, using batteries. The double-conversion type converts AC to DC and back to AC so that the load never sees a disturbance.
PDU
A power distribution unit carries power from the UPS to the racks.
Power rack, sidecar
A cabinet beside the server racks that converts AC to DC for a whole row, replacing the power supply inside each server. The first DC racks of 2026 are of this type.
±400 V bipolar and 800 V monopolar
Two ways to distribute DC at 800 volts. Bipolar uses two conductors at plus and minus 400 volts relative to ground (the hyperscalers' choice, because it reuses electric-vehicle parts). Monopolar uses one conductor at 800 volts plus a return (NVIDIA's choice).
Busway, power whips
A busway is an enclosed set of copper or aluminium bars that carries large currents along a row of racks. Power whips are the flexible cables that connect a rack to the busway.
IT load
The power drawn by the servers, storage and network equipment themselves, excluding cooling and conversion losses.
HVDC
High-voltage direct current: long-distance and subsea transmission links that use DC. Converter stations at each end turn AC into DC and back.
FACTS
Flexible AC transmission systems: power electronics that stabilize voltage and power flow on transmission lines.
Silicon carbide (SiC), gallium nitride (GaN)
Semiconductor materials that switch high voltages faster and with lower losses than silicon. Silicon carbide suits the highest voltages; gallium nitride suits compact, fast converters.
MOSFET, JFET
Types of transistor used as power switches. The MOSFET is the standard type; the silicon-carbide JFET suits solid-state circuit breakers.
Voltage regulator, vertical power delivery
The voltage regulator is the last converter, which steps power down to below one volt at the GPU. Vertical power delivery places it directly under the GPU package so the very large current travels a short vertical path instead of across the board.
Ride-through, power smoothing
Ride-through means staying connected and stable through a brief grid fault. Power smoothing evens out the rapid swings in an AI cluster's power draw so they do not disturb the grid.
NERC, FERC, PJM
The North American Electric Reliability Corporation sets grid reliability standards. The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission is the US federal regulator for transmission and wholesale power. PJM runs the grid for 13 eastern US states and Washington, DC.
NEC, NFPA 70E, IEEE 1584
The US National Electrical Code sets installation rules and is updated every three years. NFPA 70E is the US workplace electrical-safety standard. IEEE 1584 is the standard for calculating arc-flash hazards.
IEC, UL
The International Electrotechnical Commission writes international electrical standards. UL is the main US product-safety testing and certification company.
Molded-case breaker
A compact circuit breaker in a molded insulating housing, used in switchboards and panels.
ITC, exclusion order
The US International Trade Commission rules on patent complaints about imported products. An exclusion order bars the infringing products from entering the US.
Section 232
A US trade law that lets the President set tariffs on national-security grounds.
Grain-oriented electrical steel
A special steel whose crystal structure is aligned to reduce energy loss in transformer cores.
Behind-the-meter generation
Power plants built on the customer's site instead of supply through the grid. Covered in detail on the generation page.
Book-to-bill
Orders received divided by revenue in the same period. Above 1 means the backlog is growing.
GAAP, non-GAAP
GAAP are the standard US accounting rules. Non-GAAP figures are company-adjusted, usually excluding items such as stock compensation or one-off charges.
ADR, OTC line
An American depositary receipt is a US-traded certificate that represents shares of a foreign company. Over-the-counter (OTC) lines trade outside the main exchanges and can be thinly traded.
EPS, forward P/E
Earnings per share. The forward price-to-earnings ratio (Fwd P/E) divides the share price by next fiscal year's consensus earnings per share.
2027E, FY9/27E, FY3/28E
2027E is the estimate for calendar 2027. FY9/27E is the estimate for the fiscal year that ends in September 2027, and FY3/28E for the fiscal year that ends in March 2028 (the usual Japanese fiscal year).
Consensus, mean score
The average of the analysts who cover a stock: their ratings scored from 1 (strong buy) to 5 (sell), and their 12-month price targets.
LSEG
The London Stock Exchange Group, which owns one of the two main databases of analyst estimates. The other is S&P Global.
n/m
Not meaningful, shown where earnings are negative.
OCP
The Open Compute Project, an industry group founded by Facebook (now Meta) that publishes open data-center hardware specifications.
NVIDIA generations
GB300 (Blackwell Ultra) is the current rack generation. Vera Rubin follows in 2026–27, Rubin Ultra with the Kyber rack design in 2027–28, and Feynman in 2028. Kyber racks draw several hundred kilowatts each and are designed for 800 V DC.
