    Why fabrication sits under the AI stack

    Hyperscalers order GPUs; hardware companies design them. Fabrication is the factory layer — the plants, tools, and materials that turn designs into working silicon at scale.

    • Print the transistors. Advanced process nodes (roughly how small and dense the features are) need extreme ultraviolet (EUV) scanners, etch and deposition tools, and relentless yield control.
    • Assemble the system-in-package. AI accelerators glue logic dies to high-bandwidth memory (HBM) with 2.5D / 3D packaging. Capacity here — especially chip-on-wafer-on-substrate (CoWoS)-class flows — has been as tight as leading-edge wafer starts.
    • Feed the factory. Wafers, photomasks, photoresists, ultrapure gases and water, and cleanroom builders are less visible than TSMC or ASML, but every advanced line depends on them. Power devices (SiC/GaN) sit here too — they convert electricity efficiently inside datacenter racks (see also Electrification and Hardware).

    Near-term bottlenecks (orientation)

    Capacity can stay tight even when a company’s stock already reflects the story. This section is a physical supply map — not a valuation call.

    Advanced packaging — still tight into about 2027

    AI accelerators need short, dense wiring between compute dies and high-bandwidth memory (HBM). That assembly step — especially chip-on-wafer-on-substrate (CoWoS)-class flows — has repeatedly been as scarce as leading-edge wafer starts. Industry commentary still points to tightness lasting into roughly 2027 as new lines ramp.

    • TSMC (CoWoS)TSM

      Largest in-house advanced-packaging choke for AI accelerators — CoWoS and related flows that mount HBM next to logic. Expansions are underway; demand has kept the queue long.

    • Amkor TechnologyAMKR

      Outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) house adding advanced packaging capacity as an alternative / complement to foundry in-house lines.

    • ASE TechnologyASX

      Global OSAT leader in advanced packaging and assembly — another path for chiplet and HBM-class packages outside a single foundry’s walls.

    • Intel (EMIB-T)INTC

      Embedded multi-die interconnect bridge (EMIB) and the EMIB-T generation — Intel’s chiplet packaging path for tying dies together without always using a large silicon interposer like classic CoWoS.

    HBM — high-bandwidth memory supply into about 2027

    HBM stacks are the tall memory cubes glued beside AI logic. Physical supply has been tight alongside packaging; that does not by itself say any stock is cheap or expensive. Memory makers (who also appear under Hardware) include:

    • SK Hynix000660.KS / SKHY

      Historically the volume leader in HBM for AI accelerators — the memory half of many GPU packages. U.S. ADR SKHY has traded on Nasdaq since 10 Jul 2026.

    • MicronMU

      Ramping HBM share and capacity to serve the same AI packaging demand.

    • Samsung Electronics005930.KS

      Catching up / competing in HBM while also running logic foundry and packaging businesses elsewhere on this map.

    EUV tools — High-NA as a later (roughly 2028–2029) theme

    Extreme ultraviolet (EUV) scanners already print the densest layers of leading-edge AI logic. High-NA EUV (higher numerical aperture — a finer “lens” for even denser patterns) is the next tool wave. If leading-edge AI silicon stays hot, tool throughput and High-NA adoption become more important later this decade — often framed around roughly 2028–2029 — while today’s EUV installed base remains the near-term workhorse. See lithography for the company list (ASML and DUV peers).

    Who-is-who by layer

    1. Foundries

    A foundry manufactures chips designed by others (or by its own product divisions). Leading-edge AI logic is concentrated; specialty and mature nodes still matter for power, analog, and RF parts inside every rack.

    Large-scale advanced

    • TSMCTSM

      World’s largest pure-play foundry and the default home for leading-edge AI accelerators. N2 (2 nm) has been in volume production since late 2025 and is the node many 2026–27 AI accelerators target; 3 nm / 5 nm-class nodes remain large volume. Also scales advanced packaging (CoWoS) that glues logic to HBM — often the bottleneck after wafer starts.

    • Samsung Foundry005930.KS

      Advanced foundry competing at leading nodes (including 2 nm roadmaps) with large U.S. capacity plans and CHIPS Act support. Alternative capacity to TSMC for some AI and mobile customers.

    • Intel FoundryINTC

      Intel’s contract-manufacturing push (process nodes such as 18A) aiming at external AI and CPU customers alongside internal products. Execution and customer trust are the story — not just CapEx.

    • GlobalFoundriesGFS

      Specialty / mature-node foundry whose leading process is 12 nm (12LP+), plus older nodes for RF, analog, and power chips used across datacenter and networking gear — not a 3 nm AI-logic competitor. Also runs a silicon-photonics platform (Fotonix) used for optical interconnect flows.

    • Rapidus

      Japan’s government-backed 2 nm foundry entrant — building leading-edge capacity as a new regional alternative. Private.

    Mid-tier & regional

    • UMCUMC

      Taiwan foundry focused on mature nodes (often 28 nm and older) that still ship high volumes of power-management and specialty chips.

    • SMIC0981.HK

      China’s largest pure-play foundry (also 688981.SS) — strategically important inside China; export controls heavily shape what nodes and tools it can use. U.S. OTC often quoted as SMICY.

    • Hua Hong Semiconductor1347.HK

      China foundry oriented to mature nodes (also 688347.SS); part of China’s capacity consolidation story.

    • Tower SemiconductorTSEM

      Specialty foundry (analog, RF, sensors, power) with a notable relationship history involving Intel capacity.

    • DB HiTek000990.KS

      South Korea specialty foundry — signal processing and related process technologies.

    2. Lithography equipment

    Lithography prints circuit patterns onto wafers with light. EUV (wavelength ~13.5 nm) is required for today’s densest logic layers; deep ultraviolet (DUV) still does most of the other layers and older nodes.

    • ASMLASML

      Near-monopoly supplier of EUV scanners used for advanced AI logic. High-NA EUV (finer patterning) is the next capability step — more of a roughly 2028–2029 adoption story if leading-edge AI silicon stays hot; today’s EUV tools remain the near-term bottleneck. Export controls and customer CapEx cycles matter as much as technology.

    • Nikon7731.T

      DUV lithography (argon fluoride immersion and dry systems) — still essential for non-EUV layers and mature capacity.

    • Canon7751.T

      High shipment-volume lithography for older wavelengths (i-line, KrF) used heavily on mature and specialty lines.

    • Carl Zeiss SMT

      Sole maker of the extreme-ultraviolet (EUV) and High-NA optics that sit inside ASML scanners. Private; ASML holds about 24.9%.

    • Cymer (ASML)ASML

      ASML’s light-source unit — the lasers that generate deep-ultraviolet (DUV) and EUV photons for lithography tools.

    • Gigaphoton (Komatsu)6301.T

      Alternative DUV excimer light-source supplier (Komatsu subsidiary) for lithography tools. (U.S. ADR often quoted as KMTUY.)

    3. Wafer fabrication equipment (WFE)

    After lithography, tools deposit films, etch patterns, implant dopants, and clean wafers. This “wafer fab equipment” category is where Applied, Lam, Tokyo Electron, and peers earn most of their AI-cycle growth.

    Deposition & etch

    • Applied MaterialsAMAT

      Largest broad semiconductor equipment maker — deposition, epitaxy, chemical vapor deposition (CVD), and related process tools spanning logic, DRAM, and HBM lines.

    • Lam ResearchLRCX

      Etch and deposition specialist; especially important as memory stacks get taller (3D NAND, HBM) and logic features get harder to etch cleanly.

    • Tokyo Electron8035.T

      Major Japanese equipment house (coater/developers, etch, deposition) tightly tied to leading foundries and memory fabs. U.S. sponsored ADR is TELWY (replaced the older unsponsored TOELY line in Sep 2026).

    • ASM InternationalASM.AS

      Leader in atomic layer deposition (ALD) — ultra-thin, conformal films used at advanced nodes and in memory. (U.S. ADR: ASMIY — not the NYSE American miner ticker ASM.)

    • SCREEN Holdings7735.T

      Cleaning, coating, and related wet-process equipment — the unglamorous steps that protect yield. (U.S. over-the-counter line often quoted as DINRF.)

    • Kokusai Electric6525.T

      Epitaxy and deposition tools important for memory and advanced transistor structures.

    Ion implant & specialized

    • Axcelis TechnologiesACLS

      Ion implantation systems that dope silicon with precise impurities. Merger with Veeco agreed 1 Oct 2025; shareholders approved Feb 2026; China SAMR clearance still pending; close guided for the second half of 2026 (not closed as of 2 Oct 2026).

    • Veeco InstrumentsVECO

      Epitaxial deposition and process equipment — complementary to implant in compound and advanced thin-film flows. Same pending Axcelis combination (close guided 2H 2026, China SAMR pending).

    • Applied Materials (implant & CMP)AMAT

      Also leads ion-implant (VIISta) and chemical-mechanical planarization (CMP / Reflexion) tools — not only deposition. Listed again here so the implant/CMP niche is not understated.

    • Ebara6361.T

      Number-two CMP tool maker — polishing wafers flat between process layers.

    • NAURA Technology002371.SZ

      China’s largest wafer-fab equipment vendor (etch, deposition, clean) — the domestic WFE champion under export-control pressure.

    • AMEC688012.SS

      China etch and metal-organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD) leader used on logic and compound-semiconductor lines.

    • ACM ResearchACMR

      Wet-clean and plating tools used in advanced packaging and wafer processing.

    • Tokyo Seimitsu (Accretech)7729.T

      Wafer probers, CMP, and dicing equipment spanning front-end process control and back-end singulation.

    Dicing, grinding & thinning

    • DISCO6146.T

      Near-monopoly maker of precision grinders and dicers used to thin wafers and singulate dies — critical for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) stack height, CoWoS-class advanced packaging, and silicon carbide (SiC) wafering. (U.S. ADR often quoted as DSCSY.)

    4. Inspection & metrology

    Process control finds killer defects and measures whether films and features landed where they should. Without it, EUV and multi-patterning CapEx is wasted.

    • KLAKLAC

      Dominant inspection and metrology franchise for wafer process control — defect detection as nodes shrink and AI chip yields decide economics.

    • Lasertec6920.T

      Specialist in EUV photomask inspection — the masks themselves must be nearly perfect before they print billions of transistors. Also sells SiC substrate / epi defect inspection tools used on the power-device path. (U.S. ADR often quoted as LSRCY.)

    • Onto InnovationONTO

      Metrology oriented to advanced packaging and multilayer film stacks — relevant as CoWoS / HBM complexity rises.

    • CamtekCAMT

      Inspection and metrology for advanced packaging lines; extracting AI/vision features into the product set.

    • NovaNVMI

      Dimensional and materials metrology with heavy analytics — another process-control name beside KLA.

    5. Advanced packaging

    Packaging is no longer “put the die in a plastic box.” AI parts need short, dense connections between compute and HBM. Foundries do some of this in-house; specialists called outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) companies do the rest. Industry briefings still describe CoWoS-class capacity as tight into about 2027 — see Near-term bottlenecks.

    • TSMC Advanced Packaging (CoWoS)TSM

      In-house CoWoS and related flows that integrate logic with HBM. Capacity expansions have been a recurring AI-supply story alongside wafer starts.

    • ASE TechnologyASX

      Global OSAT leader with growing advanced packaging for AI and high-performance chips.

    • Amkor TechnologyAMKR

      Large U.S.-listed OSAT with full-spectrum packaging and expanding advanced / AI-oriented capacity (including U.S. footprint plans).

    • Intel (EMIB-T)INTC

      Embedded multi-die interconnect bridge (EMIB) / EMIB-T — Intel’s chiplet packaging approach as an alternative path to large-interposer CoWoS-style assembly.

    • JCET Group600584.SS

      China’s largest OSAT, investing heavily in chiplets and HBM-related packaging.

    • Tongfu Microelectronics002156.SZ

      China OSAT building multilayer / chiplet stacking capacity on a multi-year timeline.

    • Powertech Technology6239.TW

      Taiwan packaging and test house expanding 3D / AI packaging as a growth pillar.

    Hybrid bonding & HBM attach tools

    • BE Semiconductor (Besi)BESI.AS

      Leading hybrid-bonding equipment supplier for advanced stacks such as next-generation HBM and system-on-integrated-chips (SoIC)-class flows. (U.S. ADR: BESIY.)

    • Hanmi Semiconductor042700.KS

      Leading supplier of thermo-compression bonders used to stack and attach HBM dies — a tool bottleneck beside the memory wafers themselves.

    • ASMPT0522.HK

      Thermo-compression and hybrid bonders used for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) stacks and advanced packaging. (U.S. OTC often quoted as ASMVY.)

    • Kulicke & SoffaKLIC

      Fluxless thermo-compression bonding tools for advanced packaging and HBM-class attach.

    Package substrates & materials

    • Ibiden4062.T

      Leading maker of Ajinomoto build-up film (ABF)-based package substrates used under high-end graphics and AI processors.

    • Unimicron3037.TW

      Largest ABF substrate manufacturer by volume — capacity and yield here feed NVIDIA-class and peer GPU packages.

    • Ajinomoto (ABF film)2802.T

      Near-monopoly supplier of ABF build-up film — the insulating dielectric film laminated into essentially every high-end organic package substrate. (U.S. ADR often quoted as AJINY.)

    • Kinsus Interconnect3189.TW

      Taiwan ABF / package substrate supplier serving high-performance computing packages.

    • Nan Ya PCB8046.TW

      ABF package substrate maker in the Formosa group — another capacity source beside Ibiden and Unimicron.

    • AT&SATS.VI

      European high-end package substrate maker (Vienna-listed) for automotive and high-performance packages.

    • Resonac4004.T

      Packaging films (non-conductive film / underfill), CMP slurries, and electronic gases — materials that sit under HBM attach and polish steps. (U.S. ADR often quoted as SHWDY.)

    6. Semiconductor test equipment

    Automatic test equipment (ATE) proves chips work before they ship. AI parts are large, hot, and expensive — bad units hurt more.

    • Advantest6857.T

      Memory and AI-oriented test systems — often described as a test-industry counterpart to ASML’s lithography role.

    • TeradyneTER

      Broad ATE franchise across semiconductors (and other markets); a second major automatic-test platform.

    • CohuCOHU

      Test handlers and related equipment that move devices through the test floor.

    • FormFactorFORM

      Probe cards and interfaces that connect testers to wafers — capacity follows advanced packaging and foundry ramps.

    • Aehr Test SystemsAEHR

      Wafer- and package-level burn-in specialist; extraction highlights AI / SiC-related backlog and orders.

    • TechnoprobeTPRO.MI

      Top-tier probe-card maker for AI and high-performance computing logic — the interface between automatic test equipment and the wafer.

    • King Yuan Electronics (KYEC)2449.TW

      Largest independent semiconductor test house — heavy AI GPU and high-performance logic test volumes.

    • Micronics Japan6871.T

      DRAM / high-bandwidth memory (HBM) probe cards — the interface between memory testers and stacked dies.

    • MPI Corporation6223.TWO

      Probe cards and probe stations, including silicon-photonics and advanced-node applications.

    • Chunghwa Precision Test Tech6510.TWO

      Probe cards and test interfaces for logic and specialty devices.
