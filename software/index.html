AI Software — models, platforms, and apps

Hardware builds the factories; software decides what they run. This page maps the AI software landscape from the shared extraction — large language model (LLM) labs, enterprise platforms, MLOps, inference services, electronic design automation (EDA), security and governance, vertical applications, and chipmaker software stacks.

Hub section · eight layers Source: hub-extraction + data/software_companies.json Not investment advice

Why software sits on top of the AI stack

GPUs and power get most of the headlines. The software layer is where models are trained and served, where enterprises plug AI into workflows, and where chip designers simulate the next accelerator.

  • Create capability. Foundation-model labs train generative AI systems and ship them as products, APIs, or open weights.
  • Operationalize. Platforms, data tools, and MLOps products help teams label data, train, evaluate, deploy, monitor, and govern models — the difference between a demo and production.
  • Specialize, secure, and design silicon. Vertical apps wrap models for a job; security tools watch misuse and drift; EDA is the “CAD for chips” that makes AI silicon designable. Separately, chipmakers ship compilers and runtimes (CUDA, ROCm, and peers) that sit between hardware and frameworks — covered briefly here and in depth under Hardware.

Eight-layer map

  1. Foundation model developers — North America, Europe, China / Asia-Pacific.
  2. Platforms & enterprise software — data, workflow, CRM, DevOps with AI features.
  3. Infrastructure software & MLOps — hubs, labeling, experiment tracking, training clouds.
  4. Inference & serving — run models for users after training.
  5. EDA / chip design software — design and verify AI chips (fab stays under Semiconductors).
  6. AI security & governance — threats, model testing, monitoring, guardrails.
  7. Vertical / application AI — healthcare, legal & finance, code, vertical SaaS.
  8. Chipmaker AI software stacks — CUDA-class runtimes and compilers (cross-link to hardware).

Who-is-who by layer

1. Foundation model developers

Organizations that train large foundation models — especially LLMs and image models — and ship them as products, APIs, or open weights.

North America

  • OpenAI

    Frontier generative models (GPT family and related) plus ChatGPT and API products; heavy Microsoft cloud partnership for capacity.

  • Anthropic

    Claude model family with a safety-oriented research agenda (“constitutional AI”); large cloud partners among backers.

  • xAI

    Grok models plus large dedicated training buildouts (Colossus); product distribution tied to the X platform.

  • Cohere

    Enterprise-focused LLM APIs with multilingual strengths — sells models without requiring a customer to run a frontier lab.

  • AI21 Labs

    Enterprise LLMs (Jurassic family and successors) aimed at business text and document workflows.

  • Stability AI

    Open-leaning generative image and multimedia models (Stable Diffusion lineage) and related tooling.

Europe

  • Mistral AI

    French lab known for competitive open-weight and commercial LLMs; a leading European alternative to US frontier labs.

  • BigCode

    Open research collaboration on code models (StarCoder and related) — consortium-style, not a classic product company.

China & Asia-Pacific

  • DeepSeek

    Chinese lab known for high-performance models and training-efficiency claims; important for the global model race.

  • AlibabaBABA

    Qwen LLM series plus cloud AI services — models integrated with Alibaba’s cloud and consumer businesses.

  • BaiduBIDU

    Ernie model family tied to search and Chinese consumer/enterprise AI products.

  • Tencent0700.HK

    Hunyuan models inside a broad consumer and enterprise portfolio (messaging, games, cloud).

  • Zhipu AI

    ChatGLM-line conversational models — a notable private Chinese foundation-model lab.

2. Platforms & enterprise software

Large software platforms that put AI into data, workflows, customers, and developer pipelines — where most corporate AI budgets land.

  • Palantir TechnologiesPLTR

    Data and AI platforms (Foundry, AIP) for governments and companies integrating messy operational data into decision workflows.

  • SalesforceCRM

    Customer-relationship platform; Einstein and Agentforce add generative and agent features inside CRM clouds.

  • ServiceNowNOW

    Enterprise workflow / IT service platform with AI assistants for tickets, knowledge, and process automation.

  • SnowflakeSNOW

    Cloud data platform; Cortex and related features bring LLM and ML functions closer to warehouse data.

  • Databricks

    Lakehouse platform unifying data engineering and machine learning (including MLflow) for training and feature pipelines.

  • UiPathPATH

    Robotic process automation (RPA) that scripts repetitive desktop work, increasingly combined with AI document and decision steps.

  • GitLabGTLB

    DevOps platform (plan → code → CI/CD) with Duo AI features for coding and pipeline assistance.

  • Notion

    Team workspace with Notion AI for writing, summarization, and Q&A over workspace content.

  • Figma

    Collaborative design tool adding generative / AI-assisted design features for product and marketing teams.

  • Splunk (Cisco)

    Observability and security analytics with heavy ML; now part of Cisco after acquisition — still a major log/SIEM footprint.

3. Infrastructure software & MLOps

Tooling between “a model exists” and “a team can train, version, and ship models repeatedly.”

  • Hugging Face

    Open hub for model weights, datasets, and libraries (Transformers and friends), plus hosted inference.

  • Scale AI

    Data platform for high-quality labeling and evaluation sets used to train and test models.

  • Weights & Biases

    Experiment tracking and model-development tooling — log runs, compare metrics, reproduce training.

  • Labelbox

    Annotation and labeling platform for training data across vision, language, and multimodal tasks.

  • Modal

    Serverless cloud for running ML and AI jobs without managing clusters — popular with research and product teams.

  • Anyscale (Ray)

    Commercial home of the open-source Ray distributed framework used for training and serving at scale.

  • Neptune.ai

    ML metadata and experiment tracking — another experiment-store option alongside Weights & Biases.

  • Voxel51

    Computer-vision data platform (FiftyOne and related) for curating and evaluating visual datasets and models.

  • Replicate

    Hosted APIs to run open models without standing up your own serving stack.

4. Inference & serving platforms

After training, inference is running the model for users — latency, cost per token, and reliability dominate.

  • Groq

    Custom inference chip (language processing unit, or LPU) plus software stack aimed at very low-latency LLM serving. Hardware angle also belongs on the Hardware map.

  • Together AI

    Cloud for running and fine-tuning open-source models with developer APIs and optimization.

  • Fireworks AI

    Inference API focused on fast production serving of popular open and proprietary models.

  • Baseten

    Infrastructure to deploy and scale custom ML models as production endpoints.

  • DeepInfra

    Serverless inference for open models — pay-per-use endpoints without managing GPUs yourself.

  • vLLM

    Widely adopted open-source LLM serving engine (origins in UC Berkeley / LMSYS research) used under many commercial APIs.

5. EDA / chip design software

Electronic design automation (EDA) designs, simulates, and verifies chips before they go to a foundry. Foundries, wafer equipment, and packaging live under Semiconductors.

  • SynopsysSNPS

    Leading EDA and semiconductor IP supplier — synthesis, verification, and related flows for advanced logic and AI chips.

  • Cadence Design SystemsCDNS

    Peer EDA leader covering digital, analog/mixed-signal, and system design/simulation.

  • Siemens EDASIEGY

    EDA division inside Siemens (formerly Mentor Graphics) — IC design, PCB, and related verification tools.

  • AnsysANSS

    Physics and multiphysics simulation (thermal, structural, electromagnetics) used heavily in chip and system design; increasingly paired with AI-assisted design workflows.

  • OpenROAD

    Open-source academic/industry EDA flow (UC Berkeley–backed lineage) for digital chip implementation research and education.

6. AI security & governance

Protect systems that use AI — and protect AI systems themselves (prompt attacks, drift, access control, testing).

  • CrowdStrikeCRWD

    Cloud-native cybersecurity platform using ML heavily for endpoint and identity threat detection.

  • Abnormal Security

    AI-driven email and identity threat detection aimed at business email compromise and related attacks.

  • Robust Intelligence

    Testing and validation tooling for AI models before and after deployment.

  • Arthur AI

    Model monitoring and governance — track performance, drift, and policy in production.

  • Fiddler Labs

    ML monitoring and explainability for production models.

  • Galileo

    Data-quality and model-observability tooling for LLM and classical ML pipelines.

  • Rebuff AI

    Specialist defenses against prompt injection and related LLM application attacks.

  • SambaNova

Related on this hub

Software decides the workload; computation hardware runs it (optics under hardware / photonics); semiconductor fabrication builds the chips (EDA above feeds that chain); and hyperscalers buy capacity and ship cloud APIs. Weekday context: AI infra digests.

Infrastructure constraints on software economics

Training and inference cost is capped by physical bottlenecks the infra digests track — power and grid interconnect ( Electrification), high bandwidth memory (HBM) and advanced packaging ( Hardware / Semiconductors), and optical data movement ( Photonics). Software roadmaps (model size, serving latency, agent workloads) move only as fast as those layers allow.
