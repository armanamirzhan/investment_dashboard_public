AI Software — models, platforms, and apps

Hardware builds the factories; software decides what they run. This page maps the AI software stack — from large language model (LLM) labs to enterprise platforms, MLOps, inference services, electronic design automation (EDA) tools, security products, and vertical applications.

Hub section · seven layers Seed: data/software_companies.json Not investment advice

Why software sits on top of the AI stack

GPUs and power get most of the headlines. The software layer is where models are trained and served, where enterprises plug AI into workflows, and where chip designers simulate the next accelerator.

Think of three jobs software does in AI:

  • Create capability. Foundation-model labs train and release generative AI systems (chat, code, images, agents) that other products call through APIs or run on-premises / in a private cloud.
  • Operationalize. Platforms, data tools, and MLOps products help teams label data, train, evaluate, deploy, monitor, and govern models — the difference between a demo and a production system.
  • Specialize and secure. Vertical apps wrap models for law, coding, sales, and other domains. Security and governance tools watch for misuse, drift, and prompt attacks. Separately, EDA software is the “CAD for chips” that makes AI silicon designable at all.

Seven-layer map

Working top-down from “who builds the models” to “who wraps them for a job,” plus the EDA tools that sit beside silicon design.

  1. Foundation model developers — labs that train frontier and open-weight LLMs and related models.
  2. Platforms & enterprise software — data, workflow, and CRM platforms adding AI features at scale.
  3. Infrastructure software & MLOps — hubs, labeling, experiment tracking, and training tooling.
  4. Inference & serving — run models fast and cheap once they exist.
  5. EDA / chip design software — tools to design and verify AI chips (owned here; fab stays under Semiconductors).
  6. AI security & governance — detect threats, monitor models, and control risk.
  7. Vertical / application AI — domain products (legal, coding, and similar).

Who-is-who by layer

Seed names from data/software_companies.json, rewritten as plain “what they do” notes.

1. Foundation model developers

Organizations that train large foundation models — especially LLMs — and ship them as products, APIs, or open weights. Hardware and power intensive; software IP and research talent are the scarce assets.

  • OpenAI

    Builds frontier generative models (the GPT family and related systems) and consumer/enterprise products such as ChatGPT; closely tied to Microsoft for cloud capacity.

  • Anthropic

    Develops the Claude model family with a safety-oriented research agenda (“constitutional AI”); backed in part by large cloud partners.

  • xAI

    Trains Grok models and is building large dedicated training capacity (Colossus); product distribution leans on the X platform.

  • Mistral AI

    French lab known for competitive open-weight and commercial LLMs; positioned as a European alternative to US frontier labs.

  • DeepSeek

    Chinese lab that has released high-performance models with an emphasis on training efficiency; important for understanding the global model race.

  • Cohere

    Enterprise-focused LLM APIs with multilingual strengths — sells to companies that want models without building a frontier lab.

2. Platforms & enterprise software

Large software platforms that put AI into data, workflows, customer systems, and automation — where most corporate AI budgets actually land.

  • Palantir TechnologiesPLTR

    Builds data and AI platforms (Foundry, AIP) used by governments and companies to integrate messy operational data and run decision workflows on top.

  • SalesforceCRM

    Dominant customer-relationship platform; Einstein and Agentforce add generative and agent features inside CRM and related clouds.

  • ServiceNowNOW

    Enterprise workflow / IT service platform with deep AI assistants for ticket routing, knowledge, and process automation.

  • SnowflakeSNOW

    Cloud data platform where many firms store analytics data; Cortex and related features bring LLM and ML functions closer to that data.

  • Databricks

    Lakehouse platform that unifies data engineering and machine learning (including MLflow); a common home for training and feature pipelines.

  • UiPathPATH

    Robotic process automation (RPA) software that scripts repetitive desktop/back-office work, increasingly combined with AI document and decision steps.

3. Infrastructure software & MLOps

The tooling layer between “a model exists” and “a team can train, version, and ship models repeatedly” — hubs, labeling, and experiment tracking.

  • Hugging Face

    Open hub for model weights, datasets, and libraries (Transformers and friends), plus hosted inference — the default town square for open-source ML.

  • Scale AI

    Data platform focused on high-quality labeling and evaluation data for training and testing models (autonomy, LLMs, and related).

  • Weights & Biases

    Experiment tracking and model-development tooling — logs runs, compares metrics, and helps teams reproduce training work.

4. Inference & serving platforms

Once a model is trained, inference is running it for users — latency, cost per token, and reliability matter more than training FLOPs here. Some players combine custom silicon with software; others are pure software / cloud APIs.

  • Groq

    Builds a custom inference chip (language processing unit, or LPU) plus a software stack aimed at very low-latency LLM serving.

  • Together AI

    Cloud platform for running and fine-tuning open-source models with an emphasis on developer APIs and optimization.

  • Fireworks AI

    Inference API focused on fast, production serving of popular open and proprietary models.

Open-source inference engines

Projects such as vLLM are widely used under the hood even when a commercial API sits on top. They are not companies in the seed file, but they shape cost and performance for almost every serving stack.

5. EDA / chip design software

Electronic design automation (EDA) is the software used to design, simulate, and verify chips before they go to a foundry. AI accelerators are among the hardest chips to design — so EDA demand tracks AI silicon complexity. Fabrication equipment and foundries live under Semiconductors; this layer is the design tools.

  • SynopsysSNPS

    Leading EDA and semiconductor IP supplier — synthesis, verification, and related tools used across advanced logic and AI chip programs.

  • Cadence Design SystemsCDNS

    Peer EDA leader covering digital, analog/mixed-signal, and system design/simulation flows used by chip and system companies.

6. AI security & governance

Tools that protect systems that use AI — and increasingly protect AI systems themselves (prompt injection defenses, model monitoring, access control).

  • CrowdStrikeCRWD

    Cloud-native cybersecurity platform that uses machine learning heavily for endpoint and identity threat detection; expanding into AI-era security use cases.

The broader private landscape (model testing, LLM firewalls, observability specialists) is larger than this seed; expect expansion as the hub matures.

7. Vertical / application AI

Products that wrap models for a specific job — often where software margins and distribution matter more than owning a frontier lab.

  • Harvey

    AI assistant aimed at law firms and legal workflows — research, drafting, and review inside professional practice software.

  • Cursor

    AI-native code editor that embeds coding models into the day-to-day developer workflow (completion, edits, chat over a codebase).
