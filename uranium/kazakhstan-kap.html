Kazakhstan / Kazatomprom

China offtake, equity JVs, and valuation drivers — analytical scenarios, not recommendations.

As of Sep 30, 2026
← Uranium hub KAP Not investment advice

Lead story

Kazakhstan (via Kazatomprom and its JV network) is the world’s largest primary uranium producer (~39% of global production from the Holding in 2025; KAP attributable ~20%). China is KAP’s strategically most important growth buyer through (1) long-term concentrate offtake with CNNC-group entities (CNUC / CNOL), (2) equity offtake via CGN Mining JVs (Semizbay-U, Ortalyk) plus more recent CGN/SNURDC stakes formerly held by Rosatom, and (3) logistics that favor Alashankou over Russia for China-bound cargo. Chinese customs and Kazakh stats consistently show Kazakhstan supplying roughly half to two-thirds of China’s natural uranium imports in recent years, with Namibia (Chinese equity mines) the other pillar.

Trade & corporate offtake

  • ~2023 Chinese customs: ~11.1–11.3 kt from KZ ≈ ~66% of China’s natural U imports.
  • Jan–Jul 2024 (KZ stats): China ~45% of KZ U exports; China + Russia together ~84.9%.
  • Multi-year KAP–CNOL (Jun 2023) and KAP–CNUC LTCs (Nov 2023; CNUC-2024HT-001 Nov 2025 with Alashankou delivery). Material commercial terms confidential; Aug 2026 Letter of Agreement clarified settlement only — volumes/prices unchanged.
  • EGM major-transaction votes when China deals aggregate >50% of book assets — stickiness signal, opacity on price.
  • Company frames China deals on “market terms.” Portfolio-wide: majority of revenue spot-linked; some LTC legs retain fixed components / ceilings — 1H26 Group ASP $67.88/lb vs weekly spot avg ~$86/lb.

Equity JVs with Chinese partners

  • Semizbay-U — KAP 51% / CGN Mining 49%; 2025 prod 862.2 tU (100%). Partner entitled to ~49% offtake.
  • MC Ortalyk — KAP 51% / CGN Mining 49%; 2025 prod 1,836.8 tU. CGN 49% offtake. Zhalpak plant commissioned 29 Jul 2026 (up to 500 t/y) → ramp to 900 t/y in 2027.
  • Turanium (ex Khorasan-U), Kyzylkum, Zarechnoye — Rosatom stakes sold to CGN-linked / SNURDC buyers; increases Chinese equity claim on Kazakh pounds.
  • Ulba-FA — KAP 51% / CGN partners; nameplate 200 t LEU FA; full capacity 2025; quid pro quo historically tied to Ortalyk stake (Carnegie).

Rough order-of-magnitude: Semizbay+Ortalyk ~2.7 ktU in 2025 → Chinese partner offtake ~1.3 ktU, plus separate corporate CNUC/CNOL LTCs (undisclosed volume). Equity offtake ≈ ownership %; only KAP’s attributable share / corporate sales hit KAP P&L.

Does KAP “need” +25% production?

No. China doubling reactor U requirements does not map 1:1 onto KAP output.

  • Near-term guide: 100% production 25–26.5 ktU (2025) → 27.5–29.0 (2026) ≈ +10%; attributable 13–14 → 14.5–15.5.
  • Main 2026 lift = Budenovskoye ramp — 100% offtake to Russian civil through 2026 (not China-bound).
  • If KAP took ~40% of China’s +9.5 ktU increment (~+3.8 ktU/yr), that could look like ~25% of attributable mid-guide (~15 ktU) — a share-gain scenario, not a requirement.
  • Others + inventories can fill gaps; KAP may choose higher output under “value over volume” only when contracted.

Capture scenarios (illustrative)

Assume incremental China reactor fuel demand ~+9–10 ktU by 2030 vs mid-2020s base:

  • Bull: KZ retains ~55–65% of China natural U imports; KAP sells +3–6 ktU/y incremental to China (equity + corporate) by early 2030s.
  • Base: KZ share ~45–55%; incremental KAP China sales +1.5–3.5 ktU/y by 2030.
  • Bear: KZ share drifts to ~35–45%; flat to −1–2 ktU/y China exposure as African equity + domestic + other origins fill more.

Valuation drivers (analytical — not advice)

Market snapshot context (Sep 2026): LSE GDR KAP ~$63.80 / ~$13.4 bn mkt cap (stockanalysis); 2026 guidance reiterated volumes with FX-revised KZT revenue; net cash position (net debt / Adj. EBITDA −0.15× at 30 Jun 2026). Published secondary views (UBS Buy / $90 PT summary; Metal Pilot FV commentary; etc.) exist — do not treat as consensus.

What typically moves the frame:

  • Contract book — EGM China/Russia notices; % committed vs flexible; renewal cadence.
  • ASP vs spot — lag, ceilings, fixed legs (OFR sensitivity).
  • Guidance — 100%/attributable production, sales, C1/AISC, CAPEX (Budenovskoye, Zhalpak, Inkai-3).
  • Costs — MET formula (~12.4% 1H26), sulphuric acid (+39% YoY), TQZ acid plant delay to 2H27–1Q28.
  • Geopolitics / logistics — Alashankou hub; TITR vs Russia transit for Western book; Subsoil Code leverage.
  • China equity intensity — CGN/SNURDC stakes raise strategic lock-in but also NCI share of earnings.

Order-of-magnitude sensitivity (illustrative): attributable / KAP sales ~13–14 ktU guided (~34–37 Mlb); +$10/lb on fully spot-linked volume ≈ +$340–370m revenue before ceilings (1H26 ASP rose only ~half as much as spot).

Bottom line: valuation hinges less on “will China buy uranium?” (they will) and more on how much KAP captures at what ASP, versus African Chinese equity mines and domestic Chinese supply, amid logistics and policy risks.
