China uranium demand

Fleet path ~64→110 GW and reactor fuel requirements ~11.5→21 ktU — with stockpile and import nuance.

As of Sep 30, 2026
← Uranium hub Not investment advice

Capacity path

  • End-2025: 59 operable units, 62.52 GWe rated (China Nuclear Energy Association via NNSA).
  • Mid–late 2026: ~63 units / ~64.0 GWe operable; ~39 units / ~40.9 GWe under construction (WNA Reactor Database).
  • 2030 target: 110 GWe in the 15th Five-Year Plan (approved Mar 2026; WNA / CSIS).
  • Ratio: ~59–64 → 110 GW is about ~1.8×. Hitting the target from ~64 GW requires adding ~46 GW in ~4 years — aggressive; China missed the prior 70 GW end-2025 goal (~62.5 actual).

Uranium demand (~11.5 → ~21 ktU)

  • Recent / now: reactor natural-U demand on the order of ~11–11.5 ktU/yr (WNA >11 ktU in 2023; UxC via Wire China / Sightline ~11.5 ktU).
  • ~2030: UxC-linked outlook ~21 ktU — ratio ~1.83×, nearly double.
  • Increment: about +9–10 ktU/yr versus the mid-2020s base.
  • Rule of thumb: ~150–200 tU per GWe-year for steady-state PWR reloads; 110 GW × ~180 tU ≈ ~20 ktU — consistent with ~21 ktU.
  • Longer path (WNA): policy-case style figures >40 ktU by 2040 — a further step-up beyond 2030.

Why GW ×2 ≠ U demand ×2 exactly

  • First cores vs reloads — each new unit needs ~1.5–2× a normal annual reload in the first load, so build waves temporarily boost U demand above GW-proportional steady state.
  • Generation, not nameplate alone — demand tracks GWh / capacity factor.
  • Enrichment tails assay — lower tails ⇒ less natural U per enriched product (more SWU).
  • MOX / recycle — still small for China through 2030; not a material offset yet.
  • Domestic mine growth — policy aspiration ~⅓ domestic; actual domestic output still ~1.7–1.8 ktU/yr historically — hard to scale fast enough to erase import need.

Bottom line: treat ~double U requirements by 2030 as the right order of magnitude if 110 GW is hit; nuances move the number by a few ktU, not by half.

Stockpile & import nuance

  • Inventories: estimated ~132 ktU (2022, WNA via Sightline / Wire China) — among the world’s largest buffers.
  • 2025 imports: ~27,440 t uranium resources (+46.7% YoY per CRI) vs reactor need ~11–12 kt — heavy stockbuilding / equity repatriation, so imports can exceed reactor demand.
  • ~2024 natural U origins (customs compilations): Kazakhstan ~11.3 kt and Namibia ~6.5 kt as the two main pillars.
  • Self-sufficiency: China is not self-sufficient in mined uranium (~80%+ import dependence in academic estimates). It is largely self-sufficient in conversion, enrichment, and fabrication relative to the mined-U bottleneck. Stated strategy remains roughly ⅓ domestic : ⅓ overseas equity : ⅓ open market — domestic third not yet achieved.
  • Later stockpile drawdown could mute primary buying for 1–2 years; inventories do not permanently replace mines.

Who fills the +9–10 ktU increment?

Illustrative allocation (not a forecast):

  • KAP / Kazakhstan — +1.5–3.5 ktU base / +3–6 bull toward China-bound flows (corporate LTCs + CGN equity offtake). See Kazakhstan / KAP.
  • Namibia (Chinese equity) — typically +2–4 ktU China-accessible; ≤~5 optimistic country increment. See Namibia supply.
  • China domestic — maybe +1–3 ktU if Ordos/ISL ramps.
  • Other (Uzbekistan, Canada, Australia, spot, swaps) — +1–3 ktU lumpy diversification.
