Electrification — what matters most

AI halls draw so much continuous power that the old multi-step alternating current (AC) chain wastes heat and space. The industry is moving toward higher-voltage direct current (DC) inside the building — typically about 800 volts DC on the hall bus — so fewer conversion steps sit between the utility and the graphics processing unit (GPU).

You can think of three overlapping moves:

  • Near the rack (now → 2027): A hybrid “power rack” or sidecar converts facility power to 800 volts DC for dense AI rows without rebuilding the whole building. NVIDIA’s Vera Rubin platform can take this as an optional setup in the second half of 2026; broader rack use rises with Rubin Ultra from the second half of 2027.
  • Across the hall (2027 → ~2029): Row power centers and overhead DC busways feed multiple megawatts per row. DC breakers and certified solid-state transformers still constrain how fast this spreads.
  • At the fence line (always): Utility interconnect queues and slow conventional transformers remain the schedule risk. Onsite fuel cells or turbines buy time-to-power when the grid is late.

When 800 volts direct current becomes the default for new halls

H2 2026
Optional 800 V power racks into existing alternating-current buildings (Vera Rubin era).
H2 2027–2028

~2029+
New campuses designed primarily around facility-scale 800 V direct current; U.S. electrical-code support aims fuller coverage around the 2029 code cycle.

Solid-state transformers

A solid-state transformer (SST) is a power-electronics box that can turn medium-voltage grid power toward about 800 volts direct current (DC) with fewer classic conversion steps than a traditional iron-core transformer. Safety certification and skilled install crews still constrain how fast SSTs roll out.

GE Vernova and Siemens Energy have later or group SST optionality — see Facility transformers.

  • Eaton ETN Accumulate Gold

    Medium-voltage solid-state transformer aimed at 800 V halls, with a September 2026 silicon carbide deal with Infineon. Quality industrial compounder; certification and high-voltage chip supply remain industry bottlenecks.

Switchgear and DC protection

Switchgear and circuit breakers safely isolate faults so a short does not cascade through the hall. Direct-current faults are harder to clear than alternating current (AC) faults, so DC-rated and solid-state breakers matter for 800 V halls.

ABB publishes solid-state DC breakers in its Infinitus path (see SST section). GE Vernova’s medium-voltage switchgear franchise sits with classic grid hookup — see Facility transformers.

  • Schneider Electric SBGSY Accumulate

    Facility systems — switchgear, uninterruptible power, busway, and sidecar architectures. Broad mega-cap electrification name; AI direct-current upside is incremental, not a pure-play.

Silicon carbide power devices

Silicon carbide (SiC) chips are the high-voltage switches inside solid-state transformers and converters. “SST chips” are not a separate product line — public exposure is through power-semiconductor makers. Some vendors also sell gallium nitride (GaN) devices for related conversion steps.

This field is about devices (design wins and chip content). Substrate / wafer / fab capacity is tracked separately under Silicon carbide capacity. Specialty burn-in and stress-test gear is a third niche: SiC burn-in — AEHR vs Cohu →

  • Infineon IFNNY Accumulate Rainbow

    Makes the silicon carbide switches inside solid-state transformers and high-voltage converters. Design wins with Eaton and others are real; high-voltage device supply is still scarce. Rainbow mark: early data-center content may not be fully reflected versus the auto silicon carbide narrative.

  • STMicroelectronics STM Accumulate Rainbow

    Power chips that step 800 V direct current down toward the voltages near the processor, including public NVIDIA collaboration work. Device content, not facility gear — sensitive to semiconductor cycles. Rainbow mark: conversion content still early in reported mix.

Silicon carbide capacity (wafers & fabs)

Before a silicon carbide (SiC) MOSFET ships, someone has to grow a crystal (boule), slice and polish a wafer, grow an epitaxial layer, and run device fabrication at useful yield — often migrating from 150 mm toward 200 mm. That throughput is the capacity story: how many wafers and finished power dies the industry can actually make for EV, industrial, and AI-power converters.

Three SiC lanes on this hub: devices (Infineon / ST design wins) · this field (substrates, epi, captive fabs) · burn-in equipment (AEHR / Cohu). Names below are a research shortlist only — no gold / rainbow / stance tags until Performance tracker scores them.

  • Wolfspeed WOLF

    Vertically integrated SiC materials plus the Mohawk Valley 200 mm device fab and the John Palmour materials center — the clearest U.S. “more wafers / more fab starts” pure-play. Belongs under capacity (substrate + fab throughput), not the device-design-win field. Caveat: emerged from Chapter 11 in Sep 2025 with fresh-start accounting, Renesas equity, and covenant / dilution overhang — still listed on the NYSE as WOLF after reorganization.

Caveat: lasers, networking, and other materials dilute pure SiC torque; wafer demand still swings with EV cycles even as AI power adds a new pull.

Rack and row power

Sidecars, in-row power racks, busways, and uninterruptible power supplies (UPS) sit near the IT load so halls can take 800 volts direct current (DC) without rebuilding everything.

Delta Electronics also ships in-row 800 V racks (see Solid-state transformers).

  • Vertiv VRT Accumulate Gold

    Builds the gear next to the rack — sidecars, busways, and backup — so operators can feed 800 V direct current into dense rows without ripping out the building. Commercial portfolio aimed at the second half of 2026. Valuation already prices a lot of the AI story.

Onsite generation

Fuel cells (including solid-oxide fuel cells) and gas turbines make megawatts at the campus when the utility queue is late. They complement rack DC gear rather than replace it.

  • Bloom Energy BE Accumulate Gold

    Onsite solid-oxide fuel cells that make electricity at the campus when the utility queue is the real bottleneck. Native direct-current-friendly output pairs conceptually with high-voltage halls. Oracle-scale agreements re-rated the stock — treat volatility and dilution risk seriously.

Facility transformers and grid hookup

Classic iron-core facility and grid transformers plus electric power transmission interconnect still gate most campuses — lead times measured in years. Some vendors also publish later 800 V / solid-state transformer reference paths.

  • GE Vernova GEV Hold

    Heavy electrical backbone — transformers and switchgear — still gates most campuses. NVIDIA-aligned 800 V reference designs exist; solid-state transformer scale is later. Post-spin rerating leaves a thinner margin of safety.
