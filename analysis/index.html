Electrification — what matters most

AI halls draw so much continuous power that the old multi-step alternating-current chain wastes heat and space. The industry is moving toward higher-voltage direct current inside the building — typically about 800 volts direct current on the hall bus — so fewer conversion steps sit between the utility and the graphics processors.

You can think of three overlapping moves:

  • Near the rack (now → 2027): A hybrid “power rack” or sidecar converts facility power to 800 volts direct current for dense AI rows without rebuilding the whole building. NVIDIA’s Vera Rubin platform can take this as an optional setup in the second half of 2026; broader rack use rises with Rubin Ultra from the second half of 2027.
  • Across the hall (2027 → ~2029): Row power centers and overhead direct-current busways feed multiple megawatts per row. Direct-current breakers and certified solid-state transformers still constrain how fast this spreads.
  • At the fence line (always): Utility interconnect queues and slow conventional transformers remain the schedule risk. Onsite fuel cells or turbines buy time-to-power when the grid is late.

When 800 volts direct current becomes the default for new halls

H2 2026
Optional 800 V power racks into existing alternating-current buildings (Vera Rubin era).
H2 2027–2028
Volume rack-level adoption with higher-power Rubin Ultra–class systems.
~2029+
New campuses designed primarily around facility-scale 800 V direct current; U.S. electrical-code support aims fuller coverage around the 2029 code cycle.

Where the rollout can stall

  • Direct-current protection: Ordinary alternating-current breakers cannot safely clear a direct-current fault. Solid-state or direct-current-rated breakers have to ship in volume.
  • Solid-state transformers: These power-electronics boxes can turn medium-voltage grid power straight toward 800 volts direct current, but safety certification and skilled install crews are still catching up.
  • High-voltage silicon carbide chips: The switches inside those transformers are still scarce at the highest voltages.
  • Old iron-core transformers: Lead times measured in years keep delaying classic grid hookups — which is why onsite generation and solid-state paths get attention.

Private startups exist in this stack, but this page only tracks publicly traded names (or investable depositary receipts) you can buy in a normal brokerage account.
