Electrification — what matters most

AI halls draw so much continuous power that the old multi-step alternating current (AC) chain wastes heat and space. The industry is moving toward higher-voltage direct current (DC) inside the building — typically about 800 volts DC on the hall bus — so fewer conversion steps sit between the utility and the graphics processing unit (GPU).

You can think of three overlapping moves:

  • Near the rack (2026): A ±400 V “sidecar” / hybrid power rack is the practical path this year — feeding denser AI rows without rebuilding the hall. Native facility-wide 800 V DC is not the 2026 default. NVIDIA’s Vera Rubin platform can take higher-voltage rack power as an optional setup.
  • Rack-level 800 V (2027–28): Native / broader rack-level 800 V arrives optionally on Vera Rubin, more broadly with Rubin Ultra from 2H27, and more widely in 2028. Caveat: SemiAnalysis has reported Kyber slipping toward 2028 while NVIDIA says its roadmap is intact.
  • Facility DC + SST (~2029+): Campus-scale direct-current distribution and solid-state transformers mainly scale after about 2029. Until then, utility interconnect queues and classic transformers remain the schedule risk; onsite fuel cells or turbines buy time-to-power when the grid is late.

Solid-state transformers

A solid-state transformer (SST) is a power-electronics box that can turn medium-voltage grid power toward about 800 volts direct current (DC) with fewer classic conversion steps than a traditional iron-core transformer. Commercial SST units in revenue service were still scarce (single digits as of May 2026 per Claude research); they cost about twice a classic transformer; a UL data-center certification path has only recently been opening. SSTs replace medium-to-low-voltage distribution transformers and low-voltage switchboards — not high-voltage substations — and mainly scale after about 2029.

Switchgear and DC protection

Switchgear and circuit breakers safely isolate faults so a short does not cascade through the hall. Direct-current faults are harder to clear than alternating current (AC) faults, so DC-rated and solid-state breakers matter for 800 V halls.

ABB publishes solid-state DC breakers in its Infinitus path (see SST section). GE Vernova’s medium-voltage switchgear franchise sits with classic grid hookup — see Facility transformers.

  • Schneider Electric SBGSY Accumulate

    Facility systems — switchgear, uninterruptible power, busway, and sidecar architectures. Broad mega-cap electrification name; AI direct-current upside is incremental, not a pure-play.

Silicon carbide power devices

Silicon carbide (SiC) chips are the high-voltage switches inside solid-state transformers and converters. “SST chips” are not a separate product line — public exposure is through power-semiconductor makers. Some vendors also sell gallium nitride (GaN) devices for related conversion steps.

This field is about devices (design wins and chip content). Substrate / wafer / fab capacity is tracked separately under Silicon carbide capacity. Specialty burn-in and stress-test gear is a third niche: SiC burn-in — AEHR vs Cohu →

Silicon carbide capacity (wafers & fabs)

Before a silicon carbide (SiC) MOSFET ships, someone has to grow a crystal (boule), slice and polish a wafer, grow an epitaxial layer, and run device fabrication at useful yield — often migrating from 150 mm toward 200 mm. That throughput is the capacity story: how many wafers and finished power dies the industry can actually make for EV, industrial, and AI-power converters.

Three SiC lanes on this hub: devices (Infineon / ST design wins) · this field (substrates, epi, captive fabs) · burn-in equipment (AEHR / Cohu). Performance tracker scores are live for this six-name capacity shortlist; gold marks the two top capacity setups this pass, and there is no rainbow call.

  • onsemi ON Accumulate Gold

    onsemi's SiC angle here is captive capacity — growing crystals in Hudson, NH and pushing a full-chain 200 mm hub in the Czech Republic — so auto and industrial customers get supply assurance without betting on a distressed materials pure-play. Finished MOSFETs matter commercially, but this field scores the fab/materials footprint.

Rack and row power

Sidecars, in-row power racks, busways, and uninterruptible power supplies (UPS) sit near the IT load. In 2026 the practical path is mostly ±400 V hybrid / sidecar gear into existing AC buildings; native rack-level 800 V DC broadens in 2027–28 (optional on Vera Rubin; wider with Rubin Ultra / 2028).

Delta Electronics also ships in-row high-voltage racks (see Solid-state transformers). Claude power notes →

  • Vertiv VRT Accumulate Gold

    Builds the gear next to the rack — sidecars, busways, and backup — so operators can feed higher-voltage direct current into dense rows without ripping out the building. 2026 commercial push is the hybrid / sidecar year; broader native 800 V rack adoption is a 2027–28 story. Valuation already prices a lot of the AI narrative.
