SiC burn-in equipment — AEHR vs Cohu

A short research note on how silicon carbide (SiC) power chips get stress-tested before they ship, and which public equipment names sit near that step. Framing only — not a rating or recommendation.

As of 1 Oct 2026 Research watch Not investment advice

What burn-in is

Burn-in is a reliability screen: makers run chips at elevated temperature and/or voltage for a stretch of time so weak parts fail early — before a customer installs them in a car inverter, charger, or solid-state transformer. It is not the same as a quick functional “does it turn on?” check. Think of it as a controlled stress test that weeds out infant mortality.

In a factory, burn-in sits late in the flow — after devices are patterned on the wafer (and often after they are cut into dies or packaged) — so bad units do not waste packaging cost or show up in the field.

Why silicon carbide needs it

SiC power devices switch higher voltages and currents than everyday silicon logic chips. Auto and industrial buyers often demand near–zero-defect reliability: a latent defect that only shows up after hours under heat and voltage can be expensive when the part sits in a traction inverter or a medium-voltage converter.

SiC wafers and dies are also costly. Catching weak dice before full packaging (or early enough that yield math still works) matters more than for cheap commodity chips. That is why specialty wafer-level and die-level burn-in capacity shows up in the SiC power story — alongside the device makers named on the silicon carbide field brief.

AEHR vs Cohu — roles (research watch)

Both names touch SiC stress-test capacity, but they sit in different niches. Neither is a pure “SiC device” stock like Infineon or STMicroelectronics on the analysis brief.

Aehr Test Systems AEHR

Specialist in wafer-level and package/die burn-in systems (FOX family and related contactors). Public narrative and order history lean hard into multi-wafer SiC power-device production burn-in — stress many dice on a wafer in parallel before packaging. Niche equipment exposure to SiC (and other high-reliability) ramps rather than a broad test-floor franchise.

Cohu COHU

Broader test-floor company best known for handlers and related yield gear that move devices through test. Has expanded into SiC die-level burn-in / high-power stress-test handling (e.g. Neon configurations for singulated high-voltage SiC dies). SiC burn-in is an incremental wedge on a wider handler / inspection business — not the whole company.

Research-watch framing: useful to understand how SiC supply scales and where bottlenecks can appear. This note does not assign Buy / Accumulate / Hold tags or gold / rainbow marks — those stay with Performance-tracker handoffs on the main analysis list.

Nearby ATE — Advantest and Teradyne

Automated test equipment (ATE) giants Advantest (6857.T) and Teradyne (TER) sell the big pattern-and-measure platforms that prove chips function to a test program — memory, logic, SoC, and more. They dominate general semiconductor ATE.

  • ATE (Advantest / Teradyne): electrical test systems that apply patterns and measure responses at high throughput. Central to shipping working silicon; not the same product as long-duration high-power SiC burn-in ovens / wafer contactors.
  • Burn-in specialists / handlers (AEHR / Cohu niche): stress and move devices under heat and voltage for reliability screening. Overlap exists at the edges of a test floor, but the SiC burn-in capacity story is narrower than “owning ATE.”
