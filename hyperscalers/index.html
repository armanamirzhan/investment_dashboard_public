Hyperscalers — who builds and hosts AI capacity

Chips and software matter only if someone pays for floorspace, power, and clusters. This page maps hyperscale demand owners and capacity builders from the shared extraction — integrated cloud operators, GPU clouds (sometimes called neoclouds), and colocation / datacenter REIT landlords that host that demand.

Hub section · four layers Source: hub-extraction + data/hyperscaler_*.json Not investment advice

Why hyperscalers sit in the middle of the AI stack

Foundries make chips; hardware vendors sell boxes; software vendors train models. Hyperscalers are the large buyers and builders who turn those pieces into multi-gigawatt data centers and cloud services.

  • Own and build capacity. Integrated cloud operators (Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Meta, Oracle) spend tens to hundreds of billions of dollars a year in capital expenditure (CapEx) on land, power, cooling, and AI clusters.
  • Specialize in GPU rental. GPU cloud providers (CoreWeave, Lambda, and peers) run dense accelerator fleets — often leasing space and power from colo landlords while selling training and inference capacity to labs and enterprises.
  • Host the footprint. Colocation providers and datacenter REITs own facilities and interconnection hubs; in many deals the hyperscaler is a tenant of the colo, not the building owner.

Four-layer map

  1. Integrated cloud operators — AWS, Azure, GCP, Meta, Oracle (own/build at hyperscale CapEx).
  2. GPU cloud providers — neoclouds that specialize in rented accelerator capacity.
  3. Colocation & datacenter REITs — facility owners / landlords that often host hyperscaler tenants.
  4. Buildout tracker — CapEx and gigawatt targets from the shared buildouts seed (includes xAI Colossus).

Who-is-who by layer

1. Integrated cloud operators

Large technology companies that own or build AI data-center capacity at massive CapEx and sell infrastructure as a service, platform tools, and first-party AI products. Framing here is operator / builder, not “tenant only.”

  • MicrosoftMSFT

    Operates Azure, one of the largest cloud platforms, with a heavy AI data-center buildout and a deep partnership with OpenAI for capacity and product distribution. Extract notes cite very large 2026 CapEx plans and power constraints on unfulfilled Azure orders.

  • Amazon / AWSAMZN

    Amazon Web Services (AWS) is the long-time cloud-infrastructure leader, with a global footprint and custom AI chips (Trainium / Inferentia families) alongside merchant GPUs. Extract framing: among the largest CapEx spenders directed at AI data centers.

  • Google / AlphabetGOOGL

    Runs Google Cloud Platform (GCP) and designs Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) — custom silicon tightly coupled to Google’s AI stack. Extract notes highlight large CapEx guidance, multi-gigawatt campus plans, and unusual energy contracts (including nuclear restart and a corporate fusion power purchase agreement).

  • MetaMETA

    Builds large private AI clusters for training and serving models such as Llama, while also buying third-party GPU cloud capacity. Extract framing includes a multi-gigawatt Hyperion campus plan and large nuclear and solar power deals.

  • OracleORCL

    Expanding Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) aggressively, including the multi-site Stargate AI infrastructure partnership. Extract notes emphasize high CapEx relative to revenue and a large remaining-performance-obligation backlog.

2. GPU cloud providers (neoclouds)

Specialists that rent dense GPU (and related accelerator) capacity for training and inference. They often sit between “full hyperscaler” and “single tenant” — operating clusters while leasing buildings and megawatts from colo landlords.

  • CoreWeaveCRWV

    Public pure-play GPU cloud focused on AI workloads, with large contracted capacity and named enterprise / lab customers in the extract. Buildout tracker lists multi-gigawatt contracted targets and high CapEx intensity.

  • Lambda

    Private GPU cloud aimed at AI and machine-learning teams — training and inference capacity for enterprises that do not want to build their own clusters.

Related name outside the nine-name seed list

xAI (Colossus) appears in the buildout tracker as a large dedicated training campus (Memphis) rather than in the nine-name hyperscaler_companies.json set. Model/product context also sits under Software (foundation-model developers). See the buildout layer below.

3. Colocation & datacenter REITs

Facility owners and landlords. Hyperscalers frequently appear here as tenants (leasing power and space) even while they self-build elsewhere. These names are host infrastructure for AI workloads and interconnection, not pure “operators” in the AWS/Azure sense.

  • EquinixEQIX

    Largest global colocation provider in the extract framing — hundreds of data centers across dozens of metros, with interconnection that matters for AI and cloud networking.

  • Digital RealtyDLR

    Major datacenter REIT with a global portfolio and PlatformDIGITAL positioning for AI workloads. Extract thesis cites leasing momentum from hyperscaler demand and a multi-gigawatt construction pipeline.

4. Buildout tracker (CapEx & power)

Snapshot from data/hyperscaler_buildouts.json — CapEx (billions of US dollars) and power framing in gigawatts (GW). Figures are extract-era estimates; energy badges name the kinds of supply deals the source listed (power purchase agreements (PPAs), nuclear, solar, gas, fusion). Cross-link power and nuclear supply chain under Electrification.

  • MicrosoftMSFT

    CapEx 2026 (extract): about $110–120B (notes also cite a higher calendar-year figure). Power: roughly 2 GW added in one recent fiscal year; ~34 GW contracted clean energy toward 2027–2030. Energy badges: nuclear PPA (Three Mile Island / Crane framing), solar.

  • Amazon / AWSAMZN

    CapEx 2026 (extract): about $200B, mostly AI data-center infrastructure. Power: ~15 GW (Q3 2025 framing) toward ~31 GW by end of 2027. Energy badges: nuclear (Susquehanna), solar, gas. Multi-gigawatt sites in Indiana and Virginia called out in the source.

  • Google / AlphabetGOOGL

    CapEx 2026 (extract): about $180–190B. Power: growing fast; 2.7 GW Michigan campus alone (2026–2028 framing); chart target ~25 GW. Energy badges: nuclear (Duane Arnold restart), large solar, fusion PPA (Commonwealth Fusion Systems).

  • MetaMETA

    CapEx 2026 (extract): about $125–145B. Power: Hyperion campus framed at ~5 GW by 2030; large nuclear deal book (extract: ~6.6 GW) plus solar PPAs; also expanded third-party GPU cloud contracts (e.g. CoreWeave) alongside owned clusters.

  • Oracle / StargateORCL

    CapEx 2026 (extract): roughly $50B class, with CapEx-to-revenue among the highest in the set. Power: Stargate multi-site total framed toward ~7 GW (2027–2029). Energy badges: grid + behind-the-meter, gas.

  • CoreWeaveCRWV

    CapEx 2026 (extract): about $30–35B. Power: hundreds of megawatts live across many sites, with contracted capacity framed toward multi-gigawatts by 2027 and higher long-term chart targets. Energy badges: grid, gas.

  • xAI (Colossus)

    Dedicated training campus (Memphis) in the buildout file — CapEx extract estimate around $15B class; power from hundreds of megawatts live toward ~2 GW (2027 framing), with large on-site gas-turbine supply called out in the source.
