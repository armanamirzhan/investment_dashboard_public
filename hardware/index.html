Computation Hardware — chips, boxes, and cooling

Inside an AI data center, computation hardware is the gear that trains and runs models: graphics processing units (GPUs) and other accelerators, high-bandwidth memory (HBM), networking fabric, servers and racks, and the cooling that keeps dense racks from melting. Optical interconnects nest under Photonics.

Hub section · eight layers Seed: data/hardware_companies.json Not investment advice

Why hardware sits in the middle of the AI stack

Hyperscalers buy megawatts and square footage. Software decides the workload. Hardware is what actually does the math — and what breaks first when clusters get denser.

Think of three jobs this gear does:

  • Compute. GPUs, application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs), and server central processing units (CPUs) train and serve models. Merchant chips (sold to many buyers) sit beside custom chips designed for one cloud.
  • Move and hold data. HBM sits next to the accelerator. Networking switches and cables (electrical and optical) connect thousands of GPUs into one training job. Slow links waste expensive silicon.
  • Package and cool. Servers, racks, contract manufacturers, and liquid cooling turn chips into deployable capacity. Power delivery from the grid to the rack is covered under Electrification; chip factories live under Semiconductors.

Eight-layer map

Working from the accelerator out to the rack and the cooling loop. Deep optical detail lives on the nested Photonics page.

  1. GPUs & AI accelerators — merchant GPUs, custom ASICs, and related server CPUs.
  2. Memory (HBM / DRAM) — stacked memory that feeds accelerators, plus commodity DRAM.
  3. Networking — Ethernet and AI fabrics that stitch GPUs into clusters.
  4. Optical & fiber — glass and preforms that carry bits between racks and buildings (full optics map → Photonics).
  5. Connectors & cabling — high-speed electrical connectors and cable assemblies.
  6. Servers & racks — OEMs that ship AI-ready systems and liquid-cooled racks.
  7. Contract manufacturing — ODMs and electronics manufacturers that assemble white-box and branded servers.
  8. Cooling & thermal — air and liquid cooling for high-density AI halls.

Who-is-who by layer

Seed names from data/hardware_companies.json, rewritten as plain “what they do” notes.

1. GPUs & AI accelerators

Chips that do most of the matrix math for training and inference. Merchant GPUs ship to many customers; custom ASICs are designed with (or for) a single hyperscaler. The same vendors often sell the host CPUs that run the rest of the server.

  • NVIDIANVDA

    Dominant merchant AI GPU supplier (Blackwell and follow-ons) plus CUDA software and networking (InfiniBand / NVLink / Spectrum-X). Data-center GPUs are the core of most large training clusters today.

  • AMDAMD

    Credible #2 merchant AI GPU line (Instinct) plus EPYC server CPUs. Large multi-year GPU commitments with frontier labs and hyperscalers anchor the Instinct ramp.

  • BroadcomAVGO

    Leading designer of custom AI ASICs for hyperscalers (for example Google TPU and Meta MTIA programs) and of high-speed switch silicon used in AI fabrics.

  • IntelINTC

    Xeon server CPUs remain widely deployed; Gaudi AI accelerators are a smaller AI-accelerator effort beside a broader foundry and process-node turnaround story.

Hyperscalers also design in-house accelerators (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia) covered mainly under Hyperscalers. Private merchant alternatives (Cerebras, SambaNova, Tenstorrent, and similar) appear in older landscape notes and may be added here later.

2. Memory (HBM / DRAM)

High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) stacks DRAM dies beside the GPU die so the accelerator is not starved for data. The same three memory giants also supply ordinary DRAM for servers.

  • SK Hynix000660.KS

    Leading HBM supplier to major GPU programs; HBM capacity has often been sold out years ahead during AI ramps.

  • Samsung Electronics005930.KS

    World’s largest memory maker expanding HBM share while also running a large foundry business — unique memory + fab combination.

  • Micron TechnologyMU

    US memory company ramping HBM for AI GPUs and shifting more capacity toward datacenter DRAM.

3. Networking

AI clusters need fast, lossless (or nearly lossless) networks so thousands of GPUs act like one machine. Merchant switch vendors compete with silicon and full systems; GPU makers also ship proprietary fabrics.

  • Arista NetworksANET

    High-speed Ethernet switching focused on cloud and AI datacenters — strong share in hyperscaler leaf/spine fabrics and AI-oriented 400G/800G systems.

  • Cisco SystemsCSCO

    Broad networking franchise plus Silicon One chips aimed at AI fabrics; Splunk acquisition expands observability around the same clusters.

NVIDIA (InfiniBand / Spectrum-X) and Broadcom (switch ASICs) also sit in this layer — listed above under accelerators because that is how the seed file tags them.

4. Optical & fiber

As electrical copper runs out of reach and power budget, more links inside and between halls go optical — fiber, transceivers, and eventually co-packaged optics (CPO).

  • CorningGLW

    Major maker of optical fiber and preforms; large AI-related capacity expansions and partnerships (including with GPU and hyperscaler buyers) put glass at the center of optical AI buildouts.

Transceivers, lasers, silicon photonics, DSPs, CPO, and optical materials are mapped in depth on hardware / Photonics (owned by Photonics AI). This parent page keeps the fiber/glass seed name and points there for the rest.

5. Connectors & cabling

High-speed connectors and cable assemblies carry signals between boards, cages, and racks. Densities and data rates rise with each GPU generation.

  • AmphenolAPH

    Large electrical connector and interconnect supplier; datacom and AI-related demand has been a standout growth area in recent quarters.

6. Servers & racks

Original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) turn chips, boards, and cooling into branded servers and full racks that enterprises and clouds can buy or lease.

  • Super Micro ComputerSMCI

    AI server and rack specialist known for fast custom configurations and direct liquid cooling — often first with new NVIDIA GPU platforms.

  • Dell TechnologiesDELL

    Broad PowerEdge server line and enterprise support channels; AI servers are a growth slice of a larger infrastructure business.

  • HPEHPE

    Enterprise server and AI infrastructure vendor; Juniper acquisition expands the networking portfolio beside ProLiant and related systems.

7. Contract manufacturing

Electronics manufacturing services and original design manufacturers (ODMs) assemble many of the “white box” and branded AI servers that OEMs and hyperscalers ship.

  • CelesticaCLS

    Datacenter-focused electronics manufacturer building high-speed networking and AI server platforms for cloud and OEM customers.

Large Taiwan ODMs (Foxconn, Quanta, Wistron/Wiwynn, and peers) dominate volume AI server assembly in older landscape notes; expect expansion beyond this single seed name.

8. Cooling & thermal

Rack power densities for AI often need liquid cooling (cold plates, rear-door heat exchangers, or immersion) instead of air alone. Facility-scale chillers and power gear also appear under Electrification.

  • VertivVRT

    Leading thermal management and power-distribution supplier for data centers, including liquid-cooling systems sized for dense AI racks.

  • Asetek

    Specialist in direct-to-chip and related liquid-cooling loops used in AI and high-performance computing systems.
