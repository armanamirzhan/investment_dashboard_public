Computation Hardware — chips, boxes, and cooling

Inside an AI data center, computation hardware is the gear that trains and runs models: graphics processing units (GPUs) and other accelerators, server central processing units (CPUs), high-bandwidth memory (HBM), networking fabric, servers and racks, cooling, and in-rack power modules. Optical interconnects nest under Photonics.

Hub section · nine layers Sources: hub extraction + data/hardware_companies.json Not investment advice

Why hardware sits in the middle of the AI stack

Hyperscalers buy megawatts and halls. Software decides the workload. Hardware is what actually does the math — and what breaks first when clusters get denser.

Three jobs this gear does:

  • Compute. Merchant GPUs, custom application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs), and host CPUs train and serve models.
  • Move and hold data. HBM sits next to the accelerator; Ethernet and proprietary fabrics connect thousands of GPUs; optical links take over when copper runs out of reach or power budget.
  • Package, cool, and power the rack. OEMs, ODMs, liquid cooling, and board-level power modules turn chips into deployable capacity. Facility power from the grid lives under Electrification; wafer fabs and packaging under Semiconductors.

Who-is-who by layer

Expanded from the Datacenter Computation Hardware extraction, aligned with data/hardware_companies.json where present.

1. GPUs & AI accelerators

Chips that do most of the matrix math for training and inference. Merchant GPUs ship to many buyers; custom ASICs are designed with (or for) one cloud.

Merchant GPU leaders (seed)

  • NVIDIANVDA

    Dominant merchant AI GPU supplier (Blackwell / follow-ons such as Vera Rubin in the extract) plus CUDA, NVLink, and Spectrum-X / InfiniBand networking. Still the default training cluster chip for most large builds.

  • AMDAMD

    Credible #2 merchant AI GPU line (Instinct / MI-series, Helios rack-scale in the extract) plus EPYC CPUs. Large multi-year GPU commitments with labs and hyperscalers anchor share gains.

  • IntelINTC

    Xeon CPUs remain widely deployed; Gaudi AI accelerators are a smaller merchant-accelerator effort beside a broader process and foundry turnaround story.

  • QualcommQCOM

    AI200 / AI250 card and rack-scale accelerators called out in the extract for mid-decade sampling — re-entering datacenter AI silicon.

Custom ASICs & hyperscaler in-house chips

  • BroadcomAVGO

    Leading designer of custom AI ASICs for hyperscalers (Google Tensor Processing Unit (TPU), Meta MTIA, and peers) and of high-speed switch silicon for AI fabrics.

  • MarvellMRVL

    Custom ASICs and datacenter switch silicon — often cited as a second source beside Broadcom for hyperscaler programs.

  • Google (Alphabet)GOOGL

    In-house TPU line (Ironwood / 7th-gen called out in the extract) — mostly for Google Cloud and internal training, not a broad merchant GPU.

  • Amazon / AWSAMZN

    Amazon Web Services (AWS) Trainium training chips and Inferentia inference chips — vertical silicon for AWS customers.

  • MicrosoftMSFT

    Maia AI accelerator — primarily for Microsoft’s own cloud and Copilot-scale workloads.

ASIC design houses named in the extract (Alchip, Global Unichip, MediaTek) help implement those custom chips — overlap with Semiconductors / design services. Buyer context: Hyperscalers.

Private / alternative accelerators (from extraction)

  • Cerebras

    Wafer-scale engine (WSE-3) — one giant chip per wafer for certain training / inference patterns.

  • SambaNova

    Reconfigurable dataflow units aimed at agentic and inference workloads with a full software stack.

  • Tenstorrent

    RISC-V–based AI chips (Blackhole series in the extract) targeting open architectures.

  • d-Matrix

    Corsair rack-scale inference acceleration called out in the extract.

  • Groq

    Language Processing Unit (LPU) inference path; the extract notes a late-2025 NVIDIA acquisition narrative — treat ownership status as time-sensitive.

  • Graphcore

    Intelligence Processing Unit (IPU) line; extract notes SoftBank acquisition — again, verify current status.

  • IBMIBM

    Spyre accelerator called out for enterprise AI attach beside IBM’s systems business.

2. Server CPUs

The host CPU runs the operating system, networking stack, and non-GPU work. x86 still dominates many racks; Arm designs grow where power efficiency matters.

  • AMDAMD

    EPYC (Genoa / Turin and follow-ons) — strong core counts and memory bandwidth for AI hosts.

  • IntelINTC

    Xeon 6 (Granite Rapids generation in the extract) — still large installed base; competitive pressure from AMD and Arm.

  • Ampere Computing

    Arm server CPUs (Altra / AmpereOne) for cloud efficiency; extract notes SoftBank ownership since late 2025.

  • Arm Holdings

    Architecture licensor; extract mentions AGI-class high-core Arm CPU IP aimed at AI hosts.

3. Memory & storage

HBM stacks DRAM dies beside the GPU so the accelerator is not starved. Ordinary server DRAM and enterprise storage (hard drives and flash arrays) sit further out.

HBM / DRAM (seed + extraction)

  • SK Hynix000660.KS

    Leading HBM supplier to major GPU programs; HBM capacity often sold out years ahead during AI ramps.

  • Samsung Electronics005930.KS

    Largest memory maker expanding HBM share; also a major foundry — unique memory + fab combination.

  • Micron TechnologyMU

    US memory company ramping HBM for AI GPUs and shifting more capacity toward datacenter DRAM.

Enterprise storage systems (extraction)

  • Western DigitalWDC

    Nearline hard drives that hold training datasets and cold content at scale.

  • SeagateSTX

    High-capacity enterprise hard drives, including heat-assisted magnetic recording (HAMR) generations for denser nearline storage.

  • Pure StoragePSTG

    All-flash arrays for fast AI data pipelines and subscriptions around flash capacity.

  • NetAppNTAP

    Enterprise storage and data-management platforms used beside cloud and on-prem AI clusters.

4. Networking

AI clusters need fast fabrics so thousands of GPUs act like one machine. Merchant switch vendors, switch-ASIC designers, and GPU proprietary interconnects all compete here.

Ethernet switch systems (seed + extraction)

  • Arista NetworksANET

    High-speed Ethernet switching for cloud and AI datacenters — strong share in hyperscaler leaf/spine and 400G/800G AI fabrics.

  • Cisco SystemsCSCO

    Broad networking franchise plus Silicon One chips aimed at AI fabrics; Splunk expands observability around the same clusters.

  • HPE (Juniper)HPE

    Juniper Networks (acquired) adds Ethernet switching and related optics/routing beside HPE servers.

Switch ASICs, custom networking silicon & GPU fabric

  • BroadcomAVGO

    Merchant switch ASICs and Ultra Ethernet Consortium participation — silicon inside many white-box and branded AI switches.

  • MarvellMRVL

    Datacenter switch and custom ASIC silicon as a multi-source alternative for hyperscalers.

  • NVIDIANVDA

    InfiniBand (via Mellanox), NVLink / NVLink Fusion, and Spectrum-X Ethernet — proprietary and Ethernet paths for GPU clusters, including co-packaged optics (CPO) directions that overlap Photonics.

5. Optical & fiber

When copper runs out of reach or watts, links go optical — fiber, transceivers, lasers, and eventually CPO.

  • CorningGLW

    Major maker of optical fiber and preforms; large AI-related capacity expansions and hyperscaler / GPU-ecosystem partnerships.

6. Connectors & cabling

High-speed connectors and cable assemblies carry signals between boards, cages, and racks as lane rates climb (112G / 224G-class signaling in modern switch and GPU platforms).

  • AmphenolAPH

    Large electrical connector and interconnect supplier; datacom / AI demand has been a standout growth area (seed).

  • TE ConnectivityTEL

    Global connector leader with high-speed backplane and datacenter products called out in the extract.

  • Molex

    High-speed I/O and CPO-related connector work (Koch Industries subsidiary) for dense switch and GPU platforms.

  • Samtec

    Specialty high-speed connectors used in datacenter and telecom gear.

7. Servers, racks & manufacturing

Original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) brand and support systems; original design manufacturers (ODMs) and electronics manufacturers assemble much of the volume.

OEM system integrators (seed + extraction)

  • Super Micro ComputerSMCI

    AI server and rack specialist — fast custom configs and direct liquid cooling; often first with new NVIDIA platforms.

  • Dell TechnologiesDELL

    PowerEdge servers and enterprise channels; AI servers as a growth slice of a larger infrastructure business.

  • HPEHPE

    ProLiant and AI infrastructure; Juniper acquisition expands networking beside servers.

  • LenovoLNVGY

    ThinkSystem servers — value and volume positioning in enterprise AI boxes.

Contract manufacturing / ODM (seed + extraction)

  • CelesticaCLS

    Datacenter-focused electronics manufacturer for high-speed networking and AI server platforms (seed).

  • Foxconn (Hon Hai)2317.TW

    Primary high-volume assembler for major NVIDIA rack platforms (NVL-class systems in the extract).

  • Quanta Computer2382.TW

    Hyperscale server ODM — large AI server assembly volumes for cloud buyers.

  • Wistron / Wiwynn

    Enterprise and hyperscale server design/assembly; Wiwynn spun toward cloud / AI racks with fast growth in the extract.

  • Inventec / Pegatron / Compal

    Additional Taiwan ODMs assembling AI and white-box servers as capacity expands outside China.

8. Cooling & thermal

AI rack densities often need liquid cooling (cold plates, rear-door heat exchangers, or immersion) instead of air alone.

  • VertivVRT

    Leading thermal management and power-distribution supplier for data centers, including liquid-cooling packages for dense AI racks (seed).

  • Asetek

    Direct-to-chip liquid-cooling specialist for AI and high-performance computing (seed).

  • Schneider ElectricSU.PA

    Facility and IT cooling / power management; Motivair acquisition called out for liquid-cooling technology.

  • CoolIT Systems

    Direct-liquid-cooling loops used in AI / HPC servers.

  • nVentNVT

    Liquid cooling and enclosure / power solutions expanding with AI densification.

  • Delta Electronics2308.TW

    Integrated power, cooling, and datacenter infrastructure modules.

  • Johnson Controls / Carrier / STULZ / Rittal / Munters

    Chillers, computer-room air handlers, and liquid-cooling packages that keep halls within temperature limits — more facility-facing than chip-facing.

  • Submer / GRC / Iceotope

    Immersion and specialty liquid-cooling approaches for retrofit or greenfield AI halls.

9. In-rack power modules

Between the rack power distribution unit (PDU) and the GPU sits a chain of power stages — voltage regulator modules (VRMs), power-management ICs, and high-density converters. Building and campus power (transformers, switchgear, UPS) belongs under Electrification.

  • Monolithic Power SystemsMPWR

    VRM / power stages widely used on AI GPU boards (including Blackwell-class platforms in industry commentary).

  • VicorVICR

    High-density power modules and power-delivery boards for AI trays and racks.

  • Renesas6723.T

    Power-delivery modules called out in the extract for major NVIDIA GPU platforms.

  • Texas InstrumentsTXN

    Power-management ICs across servers and accelerators.

  • InfineonIFX.DE

    Power stages and silicon carbide (SiC) devices — overlaps Semiconductors’ power-semi map for discrete devices.

  • Delta Electronics2308.TW

    Also appears here for integrated rack power modules beside cooling.
